\begin{abstract}
The geometry of learned representations bears directly on when non-Euclidean distance functions help downstream. We study the metric structure of class representations, the inter-class distance matrices of frozen models, across 30 foundation models: 14 vision backbones, 9 causal language models, and 7 text embedders. Tree-likeness is measured with two zero-cost, coordinate-free diagnostics, Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity and Ollivier--Ricci curvature, calibrated on reference geometries and, crucially, read against nulls matched in dimension and covariance spectrum, since random high-dimensional clouds concentrate toward degenerate star trees and make raw $\delta$ uninterpretable on its own. Three findings emerge. First, the \emph{form} is near-universal: 69 of 72 vision model$\times$dataset combinations show tree structure beyond their matched null; its strength is determined by the training objective rather than the modality, deepens with model scale, and a parallelogram curvature-sign estimator confirms increasingly negative curvature with scale for self-supervised models. Second, the \emph{content} is model-specific: the most tree-like family (label-free DINOv2) is the least aligned with WordNet, and hyperbolic distances do not increase cross-model agreement; models converge in the form of their class geometry, not in its content. Third, the diagnostics support a training-free, two-step metric-selection protocol: $\delta$ and ORC predict when leaving Euclidean distances pays on prototype-based tasks, and the training objective selects which zero-cost metric collects the gain, cosine for self-supervised models, a parameter-free Poincar\'{e} projection for contrastive vision--language models ($+0.9$ to $+1.3$pp over cosine after L2 normalization, up to $+2.05$pp over Euclidean, McNemar $p \approx 4\times10^{-38}$). On multi-level caption hierarchies the radial axis of the projection aligns with ground-truth semantic generality, and the protocol transfers to text, correctly predicting when hyperbolic distances help little. We also report where the projection does not help, sample-level kNN and retrieval, scoping its domain of validity to prototype-based metric tasks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Semantic knowledge is hierarchical: classes nest into superclasses, concepts into broader concepts. When a frozen foundation model represents a set of classes, as image-class centroids or as embedded class descriptions, the pairwise distances between those representations form a metric space, and the structure of that space decides which distance functions serve downstream tasks well. If the inter-class metric structure is tree-like, distances respecting negative curvature or angular geometry can outperform raw Euclidean distances at zero training cost. Most work in hyperbolic deep learning \emph{imposes} hierarchical structure through specialized architectures, losses, or trainable Poincar\'{e} embeddings \citep{nickel2017poincare, Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, chen2022fully, desai2023meru}. We ask the converse, purely observational question: \emph{do frozen foundation models already organize class concepts in a tree-like metric structure, and when is that structure exploitable?}

Answering this requires care, because the na\"ive measurement is confounded. Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity \citep{Gromov1987} assigns $0$ to trees, but random high-dimensional point clouds concentrate toward equidistance, a degenerate star tree, so a low normalized $\delta$ alone is not evidence of learned hierarchy; and the reference constants themselves depend on which definition of hyperbolicity is used (the four-point constant of the hyperbolic plane is $\ln 2$, not the thin-triangles constant $\ln(1+\sqrt{2})$; \citealp{nica2016strong}).
Our measurement layer therefore rests on three legs: calibration of the exact estimator on reference geometries (trees, hyperbolic planes, spheres, matched Gaussians), \emph{excess over a null matched in dimension and covariance spectrum} as the primary reported quantity, and a direct parallelogram estimator of the curvature \emph{sign}. Together with Ollivier--Ricci curvature \citep{ollivier2009ricci} as a complementary local descriptor, this turns ``how tree-like is this representation?'' into a calibrated, falsifiable question.

Three findings emerge, which the title condenses. \textbf{Form is near-universal:} 69 of 72 vision model$\times$dataset combinations exhibit tree structure beyond their matched null, the exceptions being self-supervised DINOv2 on its pretraining-like distribution. % exp11_null_per_dataset.csv
The strength of the structure is set by the training objective rather than the modality, deepens with model scale on transfer data (DINOv2-S$\to$G excess $-0.087\to-0.146$ on CIFAR-10), and the curvature sign turns increasingly negative with scale for the same family. % exp12_curvature_sign.csv
Ablations tie the structure to learning: it vanishes under random weights, weakens under non-hierarchical fine-tuning, and emerges progressively across layers. \textbf{Content is model-specific:} the most tree-like family (label-free DINOv2) is the \emph{least} aligned with the WordNet taxonomy, ground-truth checks (WordNet correlation, superclass recovery, definition-only prompts, multi-level caption hierarchies) separate ``how tree-like'' from ``whose tree'', and hyperbolic distances do not increase cross-model agreement. Models converge in the form of their class geometry, not in its content; we discuss the relation to representational-convergence hypotheses in Section~\ref{sec:related}. \textbf{The geometry is a usable signal:} the two diagnostics, computable in seconds on training centroids, predict when non-Euclidean metrics beat Euclidean distances on prototype-based tasks, and the training objective selects \emph{which} zero-cost metric collects the gain: cosine suffices for self-supervised models, while for contrastive vision--language models a parameter-free Poincar\'{e} projection beats cosine even after L2 normalization. % exp2_metric_controls.csv, exp2b_normalized_stack.csv
We validate the resulting two-step protocol with paired statistics, transfer it to a text hierarchy where it correctly predicts marginal gains, and report the negative results that scope it: sample-level kNN and retrieval do not benefit, because the hierarchy lives at the prototype level while the radial map distorts local neighborhoods.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A calibrated measurement methodology} for tree-likeness of inter-class metric structure: estimator calibration on reference geometries, spectrum-matched nulls as the zero point, and a curvature-sign estimator, addressing the dimension confound that affects raw $\delta$-hyperbolicity readings.
\item \textbf{A geometric census of 30 foundation models} establishing that tree-like form is near-universal (69/72 cells beyond nulls), objective-determined, scale-deepening, and learned, while its content is model-specific: convergence in form, not in content.
\item \textbf{Semantic-correctness validation} against ground truth: WordNet alignment ($\rho$ up to $+0.59$), CIFAR-100 superclass recovery (ARI up to $0.61$), robustness to prompt templates including definition-only prompts with no class names, and radial alignment with semantic generality on multi-level caption hierarchies (strictly monotone chains up to $54\%$ vs.\ $4.2\%$ chance). % exp3, exp7, exp17, exp16
\item \textbf{A training-free metric-selection protocol}: when to leave Euclidean distances (predicted by $\delta$/ORC) and which zero-cost metric to use (set by the training objective), with paired confidence intervals and McNemar tests, a text-modality transfer, and explicitly scoped negative results.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperbolic representation learning.} Trees embed in the hyperbolic plane with arbitrarily low distortion \citep{sarkar2011low}, which motivated learning hierarchical embeddings directly in hyperbolic space \citep{nickel2017poincare}, hyperbolic image embeddings \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR}, fully hyperbolic architectures \citep{chen2022fully}, and contrastive image--text training on the hyperboloid \citep{desai2023meru}. All of these \emph{impose} hyperbolic structure at training time. Our question is orthogonal: we never train anything, and instead measure whether the class geometry that standard foundation models already learn is tree-like in the metric sense, and when that structure can be read out by a better distance function.

\paragraph{Emergent hierarchies in learned representations.} Closest in spirit, \citet{alper2024hierarcaps} show that image--text models encode visual-semantic hierarchies that can be traversed in embedding space, using multi-level caption data (HierarCaps). We use their test hierarchy as ground truth for one of our validations, and complement this line in three ways: our diagnostics are metric and coordinate-free (no traversal model is fit), they span three modalities and thirty models, and every reading is calibrated against a matched null.

\paragraph{Representational convergence.} The Platonic Representation Hypothesis \citep{huh2024platonic} posits that strong models converge toward a shared representation of reality, and recent work stress-tests such convergence: \citet{koepke2026cave} examine cross-modal convergence at scale, and \citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that permutation-calibrated similarity removes most apparent global convergence while local neighborhood structure survives.
Our null-calibration philosophy mirrors theirs, applied within models rather than across them, and our results land on the same side: hyperbolic distances do not increase cross-model agreement in our panel (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), so tree-likeness is a \emph{within-model} property. What is shared across models is the form of the class geometry, not its content.

\paragraph{Background: two definitions, two scales.} For a metric space $(X,d)$ and $w,x,y,z \in X$, consider the three pairings $d(w,x){+}d(y,z)$, $d(w,y){+}d(x,z)$, $d(w,z){+}d(x,y)$, sorted as $S_{\max} \ge S_{\mathrm{mid}} \ge S_{\min}$. The four-point condition defines $\delta = \sup (S_{\max}-S_{\mathrm{mid}})/2$ over quadruples; trees satisfy $\delta = 0$ exactly. Two caveats matter. (i) \emph{Definitions differ}: under the four-point condition the hyperbolic plane has $\delta = \ln 2 \approx 0.693$, while the thin-triangles constant is $\ln(1+\sqrt{2}) \approx 0.881$; the two must not be conflated \citep{nica2016strong}.
(ii) \emph{Scales differ}: absolute $\delta$ is bounded for hyperbolic spaces, so the normalized $\hat\delta = \delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ of a hyperbolic region $\to 0$ as the region grows, whereas spheres are scale-invariant and keep $\hat\delta$ high at any radius. We use the normalized scale throughout and never mix the two in one statement. Ollivier--Ricci curvature (ORC) \citep{ollivier2009ricci} is our complementary local descriptor: on a kNN graph of the class representations, the curvature of an edge compares the transport cost between the neighborhoods of its endpoints to the edge length; positive values indicate overlapping, tightly clustered neighborhoods, negative values indicate tree-like bridges.

\section{Measuring Tree-Likeness}
\label{sec:method}

This section fixes the estimator, calibrates it on reference geometries, and defines the null-corrected quantity that the rest of the paper reports. The message is that raw $\hat\delta$ is interpretable only against calibrated references and matched nulls.

\paragraph{Setup.} We analyze 30 frozen models: 14 vision backbones (supervised ViT-T/S/B/L; self-supervised DINO-B and DINOv2-S/B/L/G; contrastive CLIP-B/L, OpenCLIP-B/L, MetaCLIP-B/L, SigLIP-B), 9 causal LMs, and 7 text embedders. % TODO: final model list table in appendix
Vision class representations are class centroids of frozen features (100 training images per class) on ImageNet, CIFAR-100, CIFAR-10, DTD, FashionMNIST and MNIST; text-side class representations embed class names or descriptions. No model is fine-tuned at any point.

\paragraph{Estimator.} $\hat\delta = \delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ is estimated from $5{\times}10^5$ random quadruples, averaged over 10 seeds (s.d.\ reported); ORC uses $k{=}10$ kNN graphs. $\hat\delta$ is global and coordinate-free and connects to tree-embeddability guarantees; ORC is local and edge-level; the two fail in different ways, which is why we report both.

\paragraph{Calibration.} Table~\ref{tab:calibration} shows the estimator on reference geometries. Trees sit at $0$, hyperbolic regions low, spheres high; crucially, iid Gaussians drift toward low $\hat\delta$ as dimension grows, because random high-dimensional clouds concentrate toward equidistance, a degenerate star tree. A low raw $\hat\delta$ is therefore \emph{not} evidence of hierarchy on its own.

\paragraph{Matched nulls and excess.} For every model$\times$dataset cell we build a null cloud with the same $n$, $d$, mean and covariance \emph{spectrum} as the real class representations (Gaussian coefficients recombined with the real singular values), and report the \textbf{excess} $\hat\delta_{\mathrm{real}} - \hat\delta_{\mathrm{null}}$; a PC-permutation null (shuffling each principal component across classes) confirms the readings. Throughout the paper, ``tree-like'' means \emph{excess below zero beyond null noise} (null s.d.\ $\le 0.007$), never a low absolute value. Whitening is not a usable control here: it collapses centroids toward a near-regular simplex, itself a degenerate star.

\paragraph{Curvature sign.} $\hat\delta$ diagnoses tree-likeness, not curvature. To address the sign directly we use a parallelogram estimator: for a triangle $(b,c)$ with approximate midpoint $m$ and reference point $a$, $\xi = [\,d(a,m)^2 + d(b,c)^2/4 - (d(a,b)^2+d(a,c)^2)/2\,]/(2\,d(a,m))$, which is $0$ in Euclidean space, positive in spherical and negative in hyperbolic/tree metrics. Calibration separates the regimes cleanly: depth-9 tree $\xi=-0.24$ and $\mathbb{H}^2$ region $\xi=-0.19$ (96\% of triangles negative) versus iid Gaussian $+0.09$ and spheres $+0.11$ to $+0.12$ (0\% negative). % exp12_curvature_sign.csv
We estimate the \emph{sign}, not the magnitude, and claim nothing about a specific curvature value.

\paragraph{Projection and tasks.} The downstream probe is the parameter-free Poincar\'{e} map $\phi(x) = \tanh\!\big(\tfrac12\|s(x-\mu)\|\big)\cdot \frac{s(x-\mu)}{\|s(x-\mu)\|}$: features are centered at $\mu$, scaled so that the 95th-percentile norm lands at radius $1/\sqrt2$, and the norm is passed through $\tanh$; the direction is untouched. % TODO: diagram figure committed to 1Eqj
Tasks are nearest-centroid classification (NC; deterministic given frozen features) and 5-way 5-shot classification (FS; 1000 episodes, paired across metric arms). The metric arms are Euclidean (R), Poincar\'{e} after $\phi$ (H), the same radial map with Euclidean distances (RT; isolates the rescaling), cosine (COS; the spherical geodesic up to ranking), centered cosine (COSC), and the L2-normalize-first stack (HN/RTN). Note the distinction between deforming the \emph{configuration} (e.g.\ projecting centroids onto a sphere, which raises $\hat\delta$; Section~\ref{sec:geometry}) and changing the \emph{readout metric} on the intact configuration (COS/H), which is what the tool selects among.

\paragraph{Form: 69 of 72 cells beat their null.} Across 12 vision models and 6 datasets, 69/72 combinations show negative excess beyond null noise (Figure~\ref{fig:excess}a). The three exceptions are DINOv2-S/B/G on ImageNet, the distribution closest to their pretraining data; the same family shows the \emph{strongest} excess of the panel on transfer datasets, deepening with scale (S$\to$G on CIFAR-10: $-0.087 \to -0.146$; Figure~\ref{fig:excess}b). On ImageNet, supervised ViTs sit at excess $-0.015$ to $-0.040$ and CLIP/SigLIP at $-0.017$ to $-0.047$. Sphericizing each model's centroids \emph{raises} $\hat\delta$ for all 12 models under the spherical geodesic (10/12 under chord, the two exceptions within $.002$): the observed values are not sphere-compatible. % exp1_delta_controls.csv

\paragraph{The curvature sign agrees.} On ImageNet centroids, every model sits below the iid-Gaussian reference ($\xi = +0.09$), and the DINOv2 family crosses into negative territory monotonically with scale: $\xi = -0.020$ (S) $\to -0.070$ (G), with the fraction of negative triangles growing from 66\% to 89\%. Supervised and contrastive families remain mildly positive ($+0.02$ to $+0.04$) yet far below the sphere band. % exp12_curvature_sign.csv

\paragraph{ORC: clusters and bridges.} Mean ORC is high and positive in the most tree-like models, which is not a contradiction: an edge-type analysis on the centroid kNN graph (30 WordNet superclasses) shows that high mean ORC reflects tight \emph{within}-superclass clusters, while negative-curvature edges concentrate on the \emph{bridges} between superclasses (2--4$\times$ higher negative fraction on bridges; e.g.\ CLIP-L $.040$ within vs $.084$ across). DINOv2 on ImageNet is the exception with $\approx 0\%$ negative edges, consistent with its at-null geometry there. % exp3_orc_bridge.csv

\paragraph{Content: whose tree is it?} If the measured structure were injected by human labels, the label-free family should be the most human-aligned. The opposite holds. Spearman correlation between inter-centroid distances and WordNet tree distances on ImageNet is $+0.57$ to $+0.59$ for CLIP/SigLIP, $+0.49$ to $+0.53$ for supervised ViTs, $+0.36$ for DINO-B, and only $+0.18$ to $+0.22$ for DINOv2, with shuffle and Gaussian controls at $\approx 0$. % exp3_alignment.csv
Cutting centroid dendrograms at 20 clusters recovers the true CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI up to $0.61$ (Ward; four linkage methods checked; permuted labels $\approx 0$). % exp7_hierarchy_recovery.csv
Three controls bound the circularity concern: WordNet-coherent groupings are more tree-like than random groupings in 11/12 models (CLIP-B $.126$ vs $.174$); with a single random image per class (no pooling) distances still correlate with WordNet ($\rho$ up to $+0.34$); and the tree-likeness/alignment decoupling above is the opposite of what label injection would produce. % exp1, exp8_p6_pooling.csv
DINOv2's semantics concentrate in the angular component: sibling-vs-non-sibling triplet agreement on CIFAR-100 is $0.91/0.85$ (L/G) under cosine but only $0.71/0.63$ under raw Euclidean; strong local-angular semantics, flat global taxonomy. % exp10_local_vs_global.csv

\paragraph{Prompts do not create the structure.} Across eleven templates, six photo-style variants, a dictionary-style WordNet definition, a \emph{definition-only} template containing no class name at all, and two non-visual framings, embedder $\hat\delta$ varies by $\le 0.012$ (BGE $.115$--$.126$), and the definition-only template reproduces the baseline value. Causal LMs are template-sensitive (ranges up to $.065$), so we report their geometry as mean$\pm$range and narrow all causal-LM claims accordingly. The core vision results involve no prompts. % exp4_prompt_variation.csv, exp17_wordnet_prompts.csv

\paragraph{Multi-level ground truth.} On HierarCaps \citep{alper2024hierarcaps}, 1000 four-level caption chains, the radial axis of our projection aligns with ground-truth generality in all six text-capable models tested (three embedders and the text towers of CLIP-B/L and SigLIP-B): mean Spearman $\rho(\mathrm{level}, \mathrm{radius})$ from $+0.32$ to $+0.87$, with $9.8\%$--$53.9\%$ of chains strictly monotone against a $4.2\%$ chance rate (shuffle controls $\approx 0$), and sibling triplet agreement of $0.83$--$0.90$ (level 1) and $0.90$--$0.97$ (level 2). Both OpenAI CLIP text towers additionally show a modest negative leaf excess ($-0.010/-0.011$); SigLIP does not ($+0.014$). % exp16_hierarcaps.csv

\paragraph{Cross-model: form, not content.} Mutual-kNN alignment across all 66 pairs of the 12 vision models on the shared 1000 ImageNet centroids does not increase under Poincar\'{e} distances ($0.474$ Euclidean vs $0.427$ hyperbolic). % exp5_crossmodel_alignment.csv
Tree-likeness is a within-model property: near-universal in form (69/72), model-specific in content (each model its own tree), which is exactly why hyperbolic distances cannot buy cross-model agreement.

\section{The Tool: When to Leave Euclidean, and Which Metric Collects the Gain}
\label{sec:tool}

The geometric census supports a practical, training-free protocol with two independent steps: the diagnostics decide \emph{whether} a non-Euclidean readout will pay; the training objective decides \emph{which} one.

\paragraph{Setup.} 10 vision backbones $\times$ 6 datasets (60 cells), NC and FS as in Section~\ref{sec:method}, all metric arms computed on identical frozen features and identical episodes.

\paragraph{Step 1: when to leave Euclidean.} The $\mathrm{H{-}R}$ advantage correlates with $\hat\delta$ pooled across cells at $r=-0.45$ for NC, and within hierarchical datasets at $-0.83$ (ImageNet), $-0.87$ (CIFAR-100), $-0.63$ (CIFAR-10), $-0.46$ (DTD); for FS the within-dataset correlations are $-0.57$ to $-0.78$, while the pooled value ($-0.18$) is compressed because at this operating point almost every cell gains (56/60 positive). % table1_regenerated.csv + exp11
Because cosine is itself a non-Euclidean (angular) geometry, the sharper question is whether $\hat\delta$ predicts the advantage of the \emph{best} zero-cost metric over Euclidean: it does ($r=-0.31$ pooled, $-0.33$ within hierarchical datasets for FS; Figure~\ref{fig:bestmetric}). On the flat-hierarchy datasets the correlation vanishes for every metric (cosine gains $+0.2$pp on average on MNIST; residual offsets uncorrelated with $\hat\delta$): where there is no tree structure, no metric interversion helps, which is the negative half of the prediction. % exp2_metric_controls.csv

\paragraph{Step 2: which metric collects the gain.} The identical radial map with Euclidean distances is indistinguishable from raw ($|\mathrm{RT{-}R}| \le 0.25$pp), so the H$-$R gain is the metric, not the rescaling. Against cosine the picture splits by training objective: for DINOv2, cosine is the strongest zero-cost metric (family-mean $\mathrm{H{-}COS} = -0.5$pp, the gap widening with scale), consistent with its angular-local semantics; supervised ViTs sit at $\mathrm{H} \approx \mathrm{COS}$; for contrastive VLMs, H beats cosine by $+0.7$pp raw and $+0.9$ to $+1.3$pp after L2 normalization on CIFAR-100/10/DTD (ImageNet $+0.1$ to $+0.2$pp), i.e.\ the Poincar\'{e} readout stacks on top of normalization precisely for the family whose text towers also carry negative leaf excess. % exp2, exp2b
The largest single-cell effects concentrate where Step 1 predicts them: DINOv2-L on CIFAR-100 gains $+2.05$pp (NC), and the best zero-cost metric gains $+2.4$ to $+2.6$pp (FS) at G scale. % table1_regenerated.csv, exp2

\paragraph{Statistics.} FS gains carry per-episode paired 95\% CIs of $\pm 0.03$--$0.20$pp across the 60 cells (headline effects exceed their CI by an order of magnitude); NC is deterministic given the features, and McNemar tests on test-set decisions confirm the headline cell (242 vs 37 discordant decisions on $n{=}10^4$, $p \approx 4\times 10^{-38}$). Small effects are reported with their uncertainty rather than as headlines. % exp13_mcnemar.csv

\paragraph{Transfer to text.} Transplanting the exact NC/FS protocol to DBpedia Classes (a real 3-level hierarchy, 219 leaf classes; BGE/E5/GTE) tests the diagnostic out of modality. Geometry: genuine tree excess in all three embedders ($-0.020$ to $-0.028$) and sibling-triplet agreement of $0.88$ against the true level-2 hierarchy. Tool: these embedders sit \emph{above} the paper's $\hat\delta \lesssim 0.10$ band ($\hat\delta = 0.122$--$0.132$, ORC $\approx +0.37$), and the projection behaves exactly as predicted: no meaningful gain (FS $+0.04$ to $+0.08$pp at a $\sim$99\% ceiling, NC slightly negative). The diagnostic transfers, correctly predicting when hyperbolic distances help little, which is half of its job. % exp14_dbpedia.csv

\paragraph{Where the projection does not help.} On sample-level tasks the projection \emph{hurts}: kNN classification loses $2.6$pp on average and sample-to-sample retrieval improves in 0/60 cells. The mechanism is consistent with everything above: the hierarchy detected by the diagnostics lives at the prototype level, while the radial map distorts local sample neighborhoods. The domain of validity of the protocol is prototype-based metric tasks, and we state it as such. % exp15_verify_345.csv

\section{Ablations: The Geometry Is Learned}
\label{sec:ablations}

Three interventions tie the structure to learning rather than architecture. \textbf{Random weights}: re-initializing each backbone raises $\hat\delta$ by 65--144\%, removing the tree structure entirely. \textbf{Non-hierarchical fine-tuning}: fine-tuning on a flat objective weakens the structure. \textbf{Layer-wise emergence}: tree-likeness emerges progressively across transformer depth rather than being present at the input. All three are within-architecture comparisons at fixed dimension, so they are unaffected by the dimension confound; full curves in the appendix. % from the original submission; same-d comparisons

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the census does and does not establish.} It establishes that tree-like inter-class metric structure is the near-universal \emph{form} of class geometry in foundation models, that its strength is set by the training objective, and that it is learned. It does not establish convergence of \emph{content}: each model's tree is its own, and hyperbolic distances do not increase cross-model agreement. Claims about ``the'' geometry of foundation models should therefore be read as claims about form.

\paragraph{Excess measures existence, raw $\hat\delta$ predicts exploitability.} The two quantities dissociate in both directions: DINOv2 on ImageNet has no excess yet positive FS gains, while DBpedia embedders have genuine excess yet no meaningful gain. Raw $\hat\delta$ (with ORC) is the practical predictor of the downstream contrast; the excess is the existence proof of structure. Conflating the two overstates what either can do.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i) We estimate the curvature \emph{sign}, never a curvature value; descriptive claims are about tree-like metric structure. (ii) Causal-LM geometry is template-sensitive; those claims are reported as mean$\pm$range and remain weaker than the embedder and vision results. (iii) The class inventory is human-chosen: labels let us measure the structure (the controls in Section~\ref{sec:geometry} indicate they do not create it), but a different inventory could expose a different slice of it. (iv) The downstream scope is prototype-based metric tasks; sample-level tasks do not benefit and dense prediction, cross-modal retrieval and end-to-end hyperbolic training are out of scope (training-time approaches such as MERU are the appropriate tools there). (v) Low-dimensional visualizations are illustrations; all quantitative comparisons are made in the full feature space.

\paragraph{Future work.} Why objectives place hierarchy in the angular versus radial channel; whether tree \emph{content} converges across models with scale; the excess as a zero-cost signal of distributional novelty; and hierarchical maps that preserve local neighborhoods, which would extend the protocol beyond prototype tasks.
