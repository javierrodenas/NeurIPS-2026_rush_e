\begin{abstract}
Foundation models are often said to converge toward shared representations. We ask this question of geometry: is a model's arrangement of classes tree-like, and is that tree shared? Raw tree-likeness (Gromov $\delta$) is confounded by dimension, class count and covariance, so we build a calibrated instrument that reads every score as an excess over a random cloud with the same dimension and spectrum. Across 12 vision backbones, 6 datasets and 16 text models the instrument gives a nuanced answer. Structure beyond the random baseline is nearly universal on datasets with a class hierarchy (43 of 48 cells; 55 of 72 overall) and, in text, depends on the training recipe and emerges with scale; a star calibration shows that this structure is clustered rather than deep: models organize classes around hubs, with little hierarchy beyond, including a backbone trained in hyperbolic space. The trees are moderately shared: a naive comparison of the models' hierarchies isolates the self-supervised family as an island, an artifact of the clustering step; once controlled, every training recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision. The same calibrated scores also indicate, with modest gains, where non-Euclidean readouts help. What foundation models share is clustered structure and a partial, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy --- not one common tree.
\end{abstract}

Semantic knowledge is hierarchical: instances belong to categories (a husky is a dog), categories to superordinate classes (dogs are mammals), and so on upward. The natural geometric home for such hierarchies is hyperbolic space \citep{nickel2017poincare, sarkar2011low}, and a long line of work \emph{imposes} it at training time \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, chen2022fully, desai2023meru}. A complementary observation, latent hyperbolicity, holds that standard Euclidean models already organize concepts in a tree-like metric structure \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, yang2025hyperbolic}. Is that structure hierarchical, and for whom?

The na\"ive measurement of this question is confounded. Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity \citep{Gromov1987} assigns $0$ to trees, but three artifacts push raw readings down without any hierarchy: high-dimensional clouds concentrate toward equidistance (a degenerate star tree), anisotropic spectra mimic low-dimensional behavior, and small class counts shrink the quadruple population. These artifacts are real: small GPT-2 models sit \emph{at} their matched null despite low raw $\delta$, and classic sentence embedders share raw $\delta$ with vision-contrastive models while carrying none of their genuine structure. % exp18_text_nulls.csv

Our first contribution is an \textbf{instrument}: the exact estimator calibrated on reference geometries (trees, hyperbolic regions, spheres, Gaussians of growing dimension), with every reading expressed as an \emph{excess} over a null cloud matched in dimension and covariance spectrum and ranked against 200 null replicates. With it we ask of geometry what convergence work asks of similarity \citep{huh2024platonic, groger2026aristotelian}: what survives calibration, and is it shared --- is there a Platonic tree? They calibrate similarity across models; we calibrate geometry within a model. Comparing the resulting trees across models turns out to need a calibration of its own (\S\ref{sec:content}).

\textbf{What survives is clustered form.} Structure beyond the matched null is nearly universal on datasets with a class hierarchy (43 of 48 cells; 55 of 72 overall; negative excess in 68 of 72) and emerges with recipe and scale in text. A star calibration shows what that structure is: classes organized around hubs, with depth beyond a matched star at most marginal, including a backbone trained in hyperbolic space. % expR39b_census20_cache.csv, expR50_depth_test.csv, expR51_meru_control.csv

\textbf{Sharing is graded, and its naive measurement misleads.} A naive tree-to-tree comparison isolates the label-free family as a categorical island, an artifact of the clustering step: once the configuration is controlled, ImageNet agreement rises from $0.03$ to $0.38$. Every training recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision, and no configuration returns one common tree. % exp22, exp23_treemap_controls.npz

\textbf{Our contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A calibrated instrument for tree-likeness.} Estimator calibration on reference geometries, spectrum-matched nulls read as percentile ranks, and a class-count control. Identical raw readings receive opposite verdicts, so the raw scale is not interpretable.
\item \textbf{A calibrated census.} Genuine tree-like form in nearly every vision cell with a class hierarchy and, in text, form that depends on recipe and emerges with scale. A star calibration shows that the excess certifies clustering, not depth.
\item \textbf{The map of trees.} The naive comparison manufactures a categorical island. The controlled comparison shows graded, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy.
\item \textbf{A scoped corollary}: the same calibrated descriptors indicate where non-Euclidean readouts pay on prototype-based tasks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperbolic deep learning: imposing vs measuring.} Building on tree embeddability \citep{sarkar2011low} and Poincar\'{e} embeddings \citep{nickel2017poincare, ganea2018hyperbolic}, hyperbolic methods span vision \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, ermolov2022hyperbolic, atigh2022hyperbolic, bdeir2024fully}, vision--language \citep{desai2023meru, pal2025compositional}, graphs \citep{chami2019hyperbolic}, and language \citep{chen2022fully, he2026helm, sinha2024learning}. Latent hyperbolicity, low measured $\delta$ in standard models, has been reported for CNN/ViT features \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully}, token embeddings \citep{yang2025hyperbolic}, and word vectors \citep{tifrea2019poincare}. Those readings are sample-level and uncalibrated; transplanted to sample-level features, our instrument finds excesses mostly within null noise (Appendix~\ref{app:sample}), and our census is about inter-class (centroid) geometry.

\paragraph{Geometric analyses of representations.} Representation similarity \citep{kornblith2019cka}, intrinsic dimension \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic}, and neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} characterize Euclidean structure without testing tree-likeness; \S\ref{sec:form} relates our excess to the collapsed simplex. Estimating Gromov hyperbolicity on sampled quadruples has known bias and scale caveats \citep{fournier2015computing}, which our calibration-plus-null design addresses operationally.

\paragraph{Representational convergence.} The Platonic Representation Hypothesis \citep{huh2024platonic} argues that models converge toward shared representations; \citet{koepke2026cave} stress-test cross-modal convergence at scale, and \citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that permutation-calibrated similarity removes most apparent global convergence while local neighborhood structure survives. \citet{park2024geometry} read hierarchical concept structure in LMs from the orthogonality of concept directions; our text census is a complementary, metric-level reading of the same question. We are the geometric complement of this program: the same calibration philosophy applied within models and, in \S\ref{sec:content}, across them.

\paragraph{Background.} For four points $w,x,y,z$ of a metric space, sort the three pairing sums $d(w,x){+}d(y,z)$, $d(w,y){+}d(x,z)$, $d(w,z){+}d(x,y)$ as $S_{\max}\ge S_{\mathrm{mid}}\ge S_{\min}$; the four-point defect is $(S_{\max}-S_{\mathrm{mid}})/2$ and $\delta$ is its supremum. Trees have $\delta=0$; under this four-point definition the hyperbolic plane has $\delta=\ln 2\approx0.693$ \citep{nica2016strong}, distinct from the thin-triangles constant $\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.881$. We use the normalized $\delta_{\text{norm}}=\delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ throughout: on this scale hyperbolic regions drift to $0$ as they grow while spheres stay high, so the intuitive ordering trees $<$ hyperbolic $<$ spherical holds. Ollivier--Ricci curvature \citep{ollivier2009ricci} on a kNN graph is our local companion: positive on tightly clustered neighborhoods, negative on tree-like bridges.

\section{The Instrument}

(b)~A text embedder (BGE-base) and a vision cell (SigLIP-B on DTD) share the same raw value with opposite null verdicts. (c)~DINOv2-vs-block tree agreement before and after the configuration control of \S\ref{sec:content}. % exp1_delta_controls.csv, expR48_text_census_bs1.csv, expR39b_census20_cache.csv, exp23_treemap_controls.npz
}
\label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}
\label{sec:instrument}

This section builds the measurement layer in whose units every later claim is stated: raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is interpretable only against calibrated references and matched nulls.

\paragraph{Classes are centroids; a cell is a (model, class set) pair.} Each frozen model is a fixed feature extractor; classes are represented by centroids (vision: the mean of each class's cached training embeddings, $100$ on ImageNet, the full cached split of $80$--$6{,}000$ elsewhere; text: embeddings of ``a photo of a \{class\}'' over the 1000 ImageNet classes, plus the corpus variations of \S\ref{sec:form}). The calibrated census covers 12 vision backbones $\times$ 6 datasets (a \emph{cell} is one model$\times$dataset pair) and 16 text models (panel in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}); the task grid covers the 10 vision backbones with cached per-dataset features (all but DINO-B and SigLIP-B).
Estimator settings: the supremum four-point statistic is the pre-registered primary reading and the 99.9th percentile is reported only as robustness; $5{\times}10^5$ quadruples per seed, 10 seeds (for $C{=}10$ the $5{\times}10^5$ draws cover every quadruple many times over, so the supremum is exact); ORC on $k{=}10$ kNN graphs (ranking invariant for $k\in\{5,\dots,50\}$). On the transfer sets the excess is budget-stable from $10^5$ quadruples and image-resampling moves it far less than its size; on ImageNet every sign holds at every budget but magnitudes drift by up to $0.02$; a 99.9th-percentile variant preserves every sign (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{A low raw value is not evidence.} On reference geometries (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}) the estimator recovers the tree zero and the $\ln 2$ four-point constant of $\mathbb{H}^2$ ($\delta_{\text{norm}}\to0$ as hyperbolic regions grow), spheres stay high ($0.14$--$0.18$), and iid Gaussians drift low as $d$ grows ($0.104\to0.046$ from $d{=}192$ to $1536$): the dimension confound. A low raw value is not evidence.

\paragraph{The reading is an excess over a matched null.} For every (model, class set) cell we synthesize null clouds with the same $n$, $d$, mean and covariance \emph{spectrum} (Gaussian coefficients recombined with the real singular values; alternative constructions in Appendix~\ref{app:robust}), and report the \textbf{excess} $\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{real}}-\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{null}}$ over 200 spectrum-null replicates (20 for $\xi$). The primary evidence is the \emph{percentile rank} $r$/200, the number of replicates above the real value, and its left-tail $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\le\text{real}\})/201$; a cell is \emph{genuine} when $p\le0.05$, i.e.\ $r\ge191$. Rank-based calibration is what gives permutation scores their finite-sample validity \citep[\S5.1]{groger2026aristotelian}; a $z$-score on a handful of replicates is not rank-based, so $z$ is kept only as a secondary summary in the appendix. The null is aggregation-aware in the sense of \citet[\S5.2]{groger2026aristotelian}: the same supremum over the same quadruple budget ($5{\times}10^5$ per seed) is evaluated on every null replicate, so the max-statistic inflation is calibrated rather than ignored; the real cloud averages 10 quadruple seeds and each replicate 5 (3 in the text census), which affects variance, not bias. The instrument is a single script: given an $n{\times}d$ centroid matrix and, optionally, superclass labels, it returns the raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$, the excess with its rank and $p$ over 200 replicates, and the matched-star depth test. ``Tree-like'' below always means \emph{negative excess}, and ``genuine'' means $p\le0.05$ against the spectrum-matched Gaussian null, one hypothesis of ``no hierarchy'' (each null's scope: Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}). Sign-positive excess means \emph{less} tree-like than chance given the spectrum.

\paragraph{$\xi$ reads norm structure, not curvature.} A parallelogram estimator $\xi$ (midpoint defect of triangles) was intended as a curvature sign, but references with heterogeneous norms and no curvature drive it negative (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}); we therefore report it only as a descriptor of norm structure, never as curvature. % exp12, expR43_xi_norm_references.csv

\paragraph{Class count is a measured variable, not a nuisance.} Because $C$ sets the quadruple population, we sweep ImageNet subsets $C\in\{10,\dots,1000\}$ with two compositions: random (spanning the full hierarchy) and WordNet-coherent (siblings, effectively \emph{flat}). Results in \S\ref{sec:form}; the control makes ``needs a real hierarchy'' a measured variable.

\paragraph{One parameter-free projection and two tasks serve the corollary.} The parameter-free map $\phi(x)=\tanh(\tfrac12\|s(x-\mu)\|)\cdot\frac{s(x-\mu)}{\|s(x-\mu)\|}$ centers features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$, and preserves directions; prototypes in the ball are means of projected points re-mapped through the origin. Tasks: nearest-centroid (NC) and 5-way 5-shot (FS, 1000 paired episodes); metric arms: Euclidean (R), Poincar\'{e} (H), the radial map with Euclidean distances (RT), cosine (COS), and the L2-normalized stack. Changing the readout metric on the intact configuration, which is what the rule selects among, is distinct from deforming the configuration itself. % exp1, exp2

\paragraph{The excess certifies clustering, not depth.} The spectrum excess measures structure beyond second moments, and a pure star already has such structure: a synthetic 30-cluster star with no hierarchy scores $\approx-0.11$, as deep as any real cell (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). Depth is therefore tested separately, by comparing each backbone with a \emph{matched star} that copies its hub radius, its within-superclass spread and its cluster sizes (Table~\ref{tab:b29-depth}). The matched-star test finds residual depth marginal: on ImageNet only DINOv2-L beats its star beyond the noise, and on CIFAR-100 every backbone is \emph{less} tree-like than its star. A backbone \emph{trained} in hyperbolic space is no exception: MERU \citep{desai2023meru} and its Euclidean CLIP twin show the same clustering excess and the same absence of residual depth, and MERU's Lorentz-metric $\hat\delta$ matches its Euclidean value (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). The excess measures how classes are arranged, not the geometry they are embedded in; the class-subsampling control below measures the hierarchy's reach, and \emph{whose} tree it is is the subject of \S\ref{sec:content}. % expR42_star_calibration.csv, expR50_depth_test.csv, expR51_meru_control.csv

\paragraph{Relation to neural collapse.} Neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} predicts that the class means of a supervised classifier converge to a simplex equiangular tight frame: equal norms, equal pairwise angles. An exact ETF is the extreme case of the star caveat: all pairwise distances equal, every four-point defect zero, $\delta=0$ with no hierarchy. Two things separate our reading from it. First, the arrangement we certify is not flat: class means cluster around superclass hubs, which an ETF would erase; cutting the centroid dendrograms recovers the CIFAR-100 superclasses (\S\ref{sec:content}) and negative curvature concentrates on the bridges between clusters (Appendix~\ref{app:orc}). Second, the same clustered arrangement appears beyond the null in models trained without class labels (DINO/DINOv2, CLIP/SigLIP), on transfer datasets never used in training, and in causal LMs, outside the setting where neural collapse is stated; and the matched-star test asks what neural collapse does not, whether there is hierarchy beyond the hubs, and finds little.

\paragraph{Text: the instrument reveals what raw $\delta$ hides.} Within causal LMs, genuine form \emph{emerges with scale} in GPT-2 (Figure~\ref{fig:textnulls}): under padding-free extraction S and M are not genuine ($-0.013$/$-0.009$) while L and XL are ($-0.039$/$-0.047$; Table~\ref{tab:b23-text200}). Pythia carries genuine form at every scale tested, and so does OLMo-1B. Extraction is part of the measurement: left-padded batching alters GPT-2's hidden states and moves $\hat\delta$ by up to $0.02$, of the order of the marginal excesses (Table~\ref{tab:b28-extraction}); the padding-free protocol removes this artifact, the scale trend survives it, and individual marginal cells (GPT-2 M) do not. The rising nulls track the effective rank of the centroid covariance, and genuineness is not a proxy for anisotropy (GTE-base has the highest rank and is not genuine). % exp18b_text_anisotropy.csv, expR48b_text_census200_bs1.csv
Classic sentence embedders (BGE/GTE/E5) are not genuine despite raw values indistinguishable from vision-contrastive models whose excess is: identical raw values, opposite verdicts (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b). The same embedders show genuine excess ($-0.020$ to $-0.028$) on DBpedia, a real three-level text hierarchy: presence of form is a property of the (model, concept set) pair. The only LLM-backboned embedder tested (GTE-Qwen2-1.5B) is not genuine either, and we do not build on it (Table~\ref{tab:b23-text200}). % exp18_text_nulls.csv, exp14
Causal-LM values are template-sensitive but the scale ordering is template-robust; per-template values, ranges and the padding-free re-run are in Appendix~\ref{app:templates}. % exp4+exp17, expR49_template_nulls_bs1.csv

\section{Findings II: Shared Content, Fragile Measurement}
\label{sec:content}

If the measured trees were a shared representation of reality, models should agree with each other and with human taxonomy. Whether they appear to depends on how the trees are measured: the naive-vs-calibrated gap is the paper's thesis in miniature.

\paragraph{The naive map manufactures an island.} We extract each model's hierarchy explicitly (a dendrogram over the shared centroids) and compare hierarchies, not representations, by ARI between cut partitions (adjusted Rand index, chance-corrected partition agreement) and by the cophenetic correlation (CPCC) between tree and distances. Under the naive configuration, Euclidean distances with average linkage, the map (Figure~\ref{fig:treemap}) is spectacular: supervised ViTs and contrastive VLMs form one block (cross-family ARI up to $0.82$), while the label-free DINOv2 family appears as an island (ARI $\le 0.06$ against every supervised model). % exp22_tree_similarity_{imagenet,cifar100}.npz
The categorical island, however, is a measurement artifact. Its cuts are degenerate: DINOv2's Euclidean average-linkage dendrograms chain (up to $97\%$ of classes in one cluster), forcing ARI $\approx 0$ by construction, and Euclidean distance is the metric our diagnostics flag as mismatched for this family (\S\ref{sec:form}). % exp23_treemap_controls.npz
A configuration must be selected by a criterion fixed \emph{without reference to the agreements being compared}. Cophenetic fidelity alone re-selects the chaining configuration, so we fix: \emph{highest CPCC among configurations whose reference cuts are non-degenerate for every model} (largest cluster $\le 50\%$); it selects cosine-average on ImageNet and cosine-complete on CIFAR-100 (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). The $0.5$ degeneracy bound is the criterion's one free parameter: across admissible bounds the ImageNet gap ranges from $0.13$ to $0.38$, never back to the categorical island ($\le0.06$; Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). % threshold sensitivity, exp23_config_diagnostics.json
Under the selected configurations (Figures~\ref{fig:treemap}b and \ref{fig:treemapc100}b) the island shrinks from categorical to moderate (ImageNet $0.38$ vs block $0.48$). Across \emph{all} non-degenerate configurations DINOv2 recovers the true 20 CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI $0.2$--$0.5$, reaching the supervised band only under cosine-Ward, the configuration where the CIFAR-100 island also closes fully (per-configuration values in Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). % exp23, per-config ground-truth checks

\paragraph{The island is an artifact; sharing is graded.} Three statements hold across every non-degenerate configuration. The categorical island is an artifact. Label-free models recover the human taxonomy partially, and supervision deepens the resemblance: paired within each admissible configuration, the label-supervised ViT recovers more than each DINOv2 model in 11 of 12 comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). Tree-to-tree comparison itself is fragile to configuration. A moderate label-free gap persists on ImageNet under every configuration, consistent with the same reading: a real tree, least aligned with WordNet at the top.

\paragraph{WordNet alignment follows supervision, not tree-likeness.} Spearman correlation between inter-centroid distances and WordNet tree distances on ImageNet: $+0.57$ to $+0.59$ for CLIP/SigLIP, $+0.49$ to $+0.53$ for supervised ViTs, $+0.36$ for DINO-B, and only $+0.18$ to $+0.22$ for DINOv2, the most tree-like family, with shuffle and Gaussian controls at $\approx0$ (Table~\ref{tab:census}). % exp3_alignment.csv
Cutting centroid dendrograms recovers the 20 true CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI up to $0.61$ (permuted labels $\approx0$). % exp8_p1_recovery.csv
On HierarCaps \citep{alper2024hierarcaps} (1000 four-level caption chains), text encoders show the same reading with no class set: the projection radius orders levels within a chain, sibling triplets are resolved at $0.83$--$0.97$ (chance $0.5$), and the leaf excess repeats the text census (Table~\ref{tab:a8}). % exp16_hierarcaps.csv
Three controls bound circularity: WordNet-coherent groupings are more tree-like than random ones in 11/12 models; single-image distances still correlate with WordNet ($\rho$ up to $+0.26$); and the decoupling above is the opposite of label injection. % exp1, exp8_p6_pooling.csv
DINOv2's semantics concentrate in the angular component (sibling-triplet agreement $0.91$ under cosine vs $0.71$ Euclidean for DINOv2-L; Appendix~\ref{app:tables}), which is also why Euclidean dendrograms chain for this family and produce the naive island. The census exception is consistent with this: whatever structure DINOv2 has on ImageNet (at null under the primary statistic, genuine under the robustness one), it is the least aligned with WordNet's top level: the form-vs-content split. % exp10_local_vs_global.csv

\paragraph{The recovery is not WordNet circularity.} ImageNet classes are WordNet synsets, so recovering WordNet there could be circular. Repeating the tree map on DBpedia Classes, a three-level ontology independent of WordNet (219 leaf classes; the three text embedders), closes this door: no configuration degenerates, the embedders recover the true 70-way level-2 partition at ARI $0.21$--$0.29$ across all six configurations, and they agree with each other at cross-model ARI $0.65$--$0.82$. The recovered non-WordNet hierarchy is real, shared, and configuration-robust; the fragility of \S\ref{sec:content} is a property of the (family, concept set) pair. % exp27_dbpedia_treemap.json

\paragraph{Local agreement survives calibration.} We apply the permutation calibration of \citet{groger2026aristotelian} as they define it: for each of the 66 model pairs, $K{=}200$ permutations of the class correspondence give the null distribution, the threshold $\tau_{0.05}$ is its $\lceil 0.95(K{+}1)\rceil$-th order statistic, and the calibrated score is $\max\{(s-\tau_{0.05})/(1-\tau_{0.05}),0\}$ (scalar calibration, no layer search). Local neighborhood agreement survives: mutual-kNN is $0.472$ raw and $0.464$ calibrated, with every pair at $p=1/201$, and the null mean $0.010$ equals the analytic chance level $k/(n{-}1)=10/999$. Centroid CKA survives too ($0.633$ raw, $0.577$ calibrated, null mean $0.108$): centroids distill a genuinely shared relational base. The Poincar\'{e} reading improves neither agreement (kNN $0.472\to0.425$; CKA $0.633\to0.640$): hyperbolic distances buy no cross-model alignment, as expected since $\phi$ preserves directions (Table~\ref{tab:b31-groger}). % exp21b_local_global_K200.csv

\paragraph{Form and content anti-align.} The families with the most WordNet content (supervised ViTs, CLIP) carry the most fragile form, borne by the supremum statistic. The family with the most robust form (DINOv2) is the least aligned with WordNet's top level.

\section{Corollary: Diagnosing Exploitability}
\label{sec:corollary}

\paragraph{$\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts the gain within datasets.} Within each dataset the Poincar\'{e} advantage tracks $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ (NC $r=-0.83$ on ImageNet) and survives partialing out dimension, baseline accuracy, family and spectral covariates; without DINOv2 it holds on ImageNet and CIFAR-100 but not on CIFAR-10/DTD (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). Pooled cross-dataset correlations are family-driven and not used as evidence; on flat-label datasets no metric helps. Gains carry paired confidence intervals and a McNemar test (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). % table1_regenerated, exp20, night/correlation_cis, exp13

\paragraph{The training objective picks the metric.} The radial map with Euclidean distances changes nothing, so the gain is the metric itself, and the split follows the objective: cosine suffices for DINOv2, Poincar\'{e} $\approx$ cosine for supervised ViTs, and Poincar\'{e} beats cosine for CLIP by up to $+1.3$pp even after L2 normalization (Figure~\ref{fig:bestmetric} in Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}; held-out selection and policy comparison in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). The rule is read off the families it is scored on: it describes them rather than validating a policy. % exp2, exp2b, exp24

\paragraph{A diagnostic, not a policy.} Cosine is a safe default, gating on $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is the weakest policy tested (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}), and gains are modest and limited to prototype-based tasks (sample-level tasks never benefit, Appendix~\ref{app:sample}). On DBpedia, a three-level text hierarchy, the diagnostic predicts correctly on both sides: genuine excess, no metric gain. % exp2, exp20, exp24, exp14, exp15


\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the census establishes.} Foundation models organize classes into genuine, clustered structure and partially recover the human taxonomy, more so with supervision; they do not converge to one common tree. Form beyond matched nulls is widespread but not universal: near-universal in the vision backbones tested (ViTs and two ConvNets), recipe- and scale-dependent in text, always relative to a concept set. Its content is moderately shared and configuration-dependent: all recipes recover the human taxonomy partially under every non-degenerate configuration, the residual gap on ImageNet ranges from $0.13$ to $0.38$ with the comparison threshold, and the naive islands are a measurement artifact. Raw readings of $\delta$, of $\xi$, or of tree similarity are not interpretable without the instrument; excess measures existence while raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts exploitability, and the two dissociate in both directions (DINOv2 on ImageNet; the DBpedia embedders).

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~The spectrum excess certifies structure beyond second moments, star-like clustering included; against matched stars the residual depth is marginal (Appendix~\ref{app:star}), so the hierarchy's reach rests on the class-subsampling control and its content on \S\ref{sec:content}. (ii)~The census is class-centroid geometry on six datasets; other concept sets may expose different structure. (iii)~Causal-LM values (not the scale ordering) are template-conditioned. (iv)~The objective--geometry link is correlational (triplet-bounded, not closed); the within-dataset $\delta$--gain prediction rests chiefly on DINOv2's scale range (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). (v)~Downstream scope is prototype-based metric tasks with modest gains. (vi)~The parallelogram descriptor $\xi$ tracks norm dispersion rather than curvature (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}) and is reported only as such. (vii)~A causal LM's class representation depends on the extraction protocol as much as on the model: left-padded batching moves $\hat\delta$ by up to $0.018$, of the order of the L/XL excess.

This work analyzes publicly released models and standard public datasets, involves no human subjects and no personal data, and proposes an analysis methodology and a metric-selection heuristic. We do not foresee ethical risks beyond those already associated with the underlying models and datasets.

\subsection{Parallelogram defect $\xi$: a norm-structure descriptor}
\label{app:xi}
For a triangle $(a,b,c)$ with $m$ the approximate midpoint of $(b,c)$ (the minimizer of $D(\cdot,b){+}D(\cdot,c){+}|D(\cdot,b){-}D(\cdot,c)|$), the parallelogram defect is $\xi = \big(D(a,m)^2 + \tfrac14 D(b,c)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,b)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,c)^2\big)\,/\,(2\,D(a,m)\,\mathrm{diam})$, averaged over $2{\times}10^4$ sampled triangles: $0$ in Euclidean space, positive on spheres, negative in hyperbolic space and on trees.
