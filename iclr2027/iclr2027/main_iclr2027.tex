\begin{abstract}
Hyperbolic methods for representation learning rest on a premise: standard models are latently hyperbolic, their class geometry reading as tree-like under Gromov $\delta$. We show that this reading is confounded, by dimension, by covariance spectrum and by the supremum statistic itself, and build a calibrated instrument that reads every score as an excess over a random cloud with the same dimension and spectrum, ranked against 200 replicates, with a depth test whose power and validity regime are measured. Applied to 12 vision backbones, 6 datasets and 16 text models, the instrument gives a nuanced answer. Sample-level readings, the ones that motivate hyperbolic methods, sit within null noise in most cells; where they do not, the excess is small and concentrated in the self-supervised family. At the class level, structure beyond the null is present in most models and every family (49 of 72 cells; 18 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100), it is clustered rather than deep, and hierarchy above the superclasses is certified in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones; a backbone trained in hyperbolic space (MERU) shows the same clustering and no additional depth. The trees are moderately shared: a naive comparison isolates the self-supervised family as an island, an artifact of the clustering cut; controlled, every training recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision, and the self-supervised tree lives in angles rather than distances. What foundation models share is clustered structure and a partial, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy --- not one common tree, and not a latent hyperbolic geometry to be imposed by default. % expR62_samplelevel_record.csv, expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR63_meru_record.csv
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\textbf{Is there a Platonic tree?}\quad Read correctly, foundation models organize classes into clustered structure: occasionally hierarchical, moderately shared, supervision-dependent in content. They do not converge to one common tree, and their raw tree-likeness is not evidence for hyperbolic geometry.

Semantic knowledge is hierarchical: instances belong to categories (a husky is a dog), categories to superordinate classes (dogs are mammals), and so on upward. The natural geometric home for such hierarchies is hyperbolic space \citep{nickel2017poincare, sarkar2011low}, and a line of work \emph{imposes} it on representations at training time: in vision \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, ermolov2022hyperbolic, atigh2022hyperbolic, bdeir2024fully}, in vision--language \citep{desai2023meru, pal2025compositional} and in language \citep{chen2022fully, he2026helm, sinha2024learning}. The premise these methods cite is \emph{latent hyperbolicity}: standard models are said to organize concepts already in a tree-like metric structure, read as a low Gromov $\delta$ on their features \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully}, on token embeddings \citep{yang2025hyperbolic} and on word vectors \citep{tifrea2019poincare}. Is that reading right, and what does it license?

The reading is confounded. Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity \citep{Gromov1987} assigns $0$ to trees, but three artifacts push raw readings down without any hierarchy: high-dimensional clouds concentrate toward equidistance (a degenerate star tree), anisotropic spectra mimic low-dimensional behavior, and the supremum over sampled quadruples is a one-quadruple extreme that does not converge. The artifacts are real: small GPT-2 models sit \emph{at} their matched null despite low raw $\delta$, and classic sentence embedders share raw $\delta$ with vision-contrastive models while carrying none of their genuine structure. % exp18_text_nulls.csv, expR53_text_haar_p999_200.csv

We build the instrument that reads $\delta$ correctly: the estimator calibrated on reference geometries, every reading expressed as an \emph{excess} over a null cloud matched in dimension and covariance spectrum and ranked against 200 replicates, and a depth test whose power and validity regime are measured. We apply it at the sample level, where the premise is read, and at the class level, where hierarchy lives. With it we ask of geometry what convergence work asks of similarity \citep{huh2024platonic, groger2026aristotelian}: what survives calibration, and is it shared, is there a Platonic tree? They calibrate similarity across models; we calibrate geometry within a model, and the comparison of trees across models needs a calibration of its own (\S\ref{sec:content}).

\textbf{The answer has three parts.} Sample-level readings sit within null noise in most cells, and the calibrated excess is small where it survives: the premise does not survive calibration as stated. At the class level, structure beyond the null is present in most models and every family (49 of 72 cells; 18 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100), it is clustered rather than deep, hierarchy above the superclasses is certified in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones, and a backbone trained in hyperbolic space shows the same clustering and no additional depth. The trees are moderately shared: the naive comparison isolates the self-supervised family as an island, an artifact of the clustering cut; controlled, every recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision, and the self-supervised tree lives in angles rather than distances. What follows for practice is what to measure before imposing curvature, and which zero-cost metric collects the structure that is there. % expR62_samplelevel_record.csv, expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR63_meru_record.csv, exp23_treemap_controls.npz

\textbf{Our contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A calibrated instrument for latent hyperbolicity.} Estimator calibration, spectrum-matched nulls read as BH-corrected ranks, and a validated depth test; raw $\delta$ misleads at the sample level and at the class level alike.
\item \textbf{A calibrated census.} Clustered structure beyond the null in most models, hierarchy certified in a few, and MERU as the control for imposing the geometry.
\item \textbf{The map of trees.} The categorical island is an artifact of the cut; sharing is graded and supervision-dependent, and the self-supervised tree lives in angles.
\item \textbf{Consequences for practice.} What to measure before imposing curvature, and which zero-cost metric collects the structure that is there (details in the appendix).
\end{enumerate}


\section{Related Work and Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Latent hyperbolicity, the target claim.} Hyperbolic representation learning builds on tree embeddability \citep{sarkar2011low} and Poincar\'{e} embeddings \citep{nickel2017poincare, ganea2018hyperbolic} and now spans vision \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, ermolov2022hyperbolic, atigh2022hyperbolic, bdeir2024fully}, vision--language \citep{desai2023meru, pal2025compositional}, graphs \citep{chami2019hyperbolic} and language \citep{chen2022fully, he2026helm, sinha2024learning}. Its stated premise, that standard models are already tree-like, rests on low measured $\delta$ for CNN/ViT features \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully}, token embeddings \citep{yang2025hyperbolic} and word vectors \citep{tifrea2019poincare}. Those readings are sample-level, uncalibrated and taken under a single construction of the statistic; \S\ref{sec:form} re-reads them with the instrument.

\paragraph{Calibrating $\delta$ and geometry against nulls.} Measuring Gromov $\delta$ on networks against random graphs of matched size and degree sequence is established in network science \citep{narayan2011curvature, kennedy2013hyperbolicity, adcock2013treelike}, where raw $\delta$ was already found not to certify tree-likeness; the confound itself is therefore not our contribution. Representation similarity \citep{kornblith2019cka}, intrinsic dimension \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic} and neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} characterize Euclidean structure without testing tree-likeness, and neural collapse is the flat limit our reading is compared with in \S\ref{sec:form}. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis \citep{huh2024platonic} argues that models converge toward shared representations; \citet{koepke2026cave} stress-test cross-modal convergence, \citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that permutation-calibrated similarity removes most apparent global convergence while local structure survives, and \citet{park2024geometry} read hierarchical concept structure in LMs from orthogonality. We are the geometric complement of that program. What is new here is the instrument for embedding clouds rather than graphs: spectrum-matched nulls at the resolution of the null replicate, a depth test against matched stars with measured power, a hyperbolic backbone as the control for imposing the geometry, and a calibration of the tree-to-tree comparison itself.

\paragraph{Background.} For four points $w,x,y,z$ of a metric space, sort the three pairing sums $d(w,x){+}d(y,z)$, $d(w,y){+}d(x,z)$, $d(w,z){+}d(x,y)$ as $S_{\max}\ge S_{\mathrm{mid}}\ge S_{\min}$; the four-point defect is $(S_{\max}-S_{\mathrm{mid}})/2$ and $\delta$ is its supremum. Trees have $\delta=0$; under this definition the hyperbolic plane has $\delta=\ln 2\approx0.693$ \citep{nica2016strong}, distinct from the thin-triangles constant $\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.881$. We use the normalized $\delta_{\text{norm}}=\delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ throughout: hyperbolic regions drift to $0$ as they grow while spheres stay high, so the ordering trees $<$ hyperbolic $<$ spherical holds; sampled-quadruple estimation has known bias and scale caveats \citep{fournier2015computing}, which the calibration-plus-null design addresses operationally. Ollivier--Ricci curvature \citep{ollivier2009ricci} on a kNN graph is the local companion, positive within clusters and negative on bridges (Appendix~\ref{app:orc}).

This section builds the measurement layer in whose units every later claim is stated: raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is interpretable only against calibrated references and matched nulls.

\paragraph{Where the premise is read, and where hierarchy is measured.} Each frozen model is a fixed feature extractor. The premise of latent hyperbolicity is read at the \emph{sample level}, on per-image features, which is where the literature measures it; hierarchy is measured at the \emph{class level}, on centroids (vision: the mean of each class's cached training embeddings, $100$ on ImageNet, the full cached split of $80$--$6{,}000$ elsewhere; text: embeddings of ``a photo of a \{class\}'' over the 1000 ImageNet classes, plus the corpus variations of \S\ref{sec:content}). A \emph{cell} is one (model, class set) pair; the census covers 12 vision backbones $\times$ 6 datasets and 16 text models, and the task grid the 10 backbones with cached per-dataset features (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).
Estimator settings: the 99.9th-percentile four-point statistic $\hat\delta_{99.9}$ is the reading of record and the supremum, the pre-registered reading, is reported in the appendix (the reason is stated below); $5{\times}10^5$ quadruples per seed, 10 seeds (exact at $C{=}10$, where the draws cover every quadruple many times over). On the transfer sets the excess is budget-stable from $10^5$ quadruples and image-resampling moves it far less than its size; on ImageNet every sign holds at every budget but magnitudes drift by up to $0.02$ (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{A low raw value is not evidence.} On reference geometries (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}) the estimator recovers the tree zero and the $\ln 2$ four-point constant of $\mathbb{H}^2$ ($\delta_{\text{norm}}\to0$ as hyperbolic regions grow), spheres stay high ($0.14$--$0.18$), and iid Gaussians drift low as $d$ grows ($0.104\to0.046$ from $d{=}192$ to $1536$): the dimension confound.

\paragraph{The reading is an excess over a matched null.} For every cell we synthesize null clouds with the same $n$, $d$, mean and covariance \emph{spectrum} (the Haar construction: random orthogonal coefficients recombined with the real singular values, so the sample spectrum is exact; the Gaussian-coefficient construction is the alternative reported in Appendix~\ref{app:robust}), and report the \textbf{excess} $\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{real}}-\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{null}}$ over 200 spectrum-null replicates. The primary evidence is the \emph{percentile rank} $r$/200, the number of replicates above the real value, and its left-tail $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\le\text{real}\})/201$; a cell is \emph{genuine} when its Benjamini--Hochberg-corrected $p$ across the census is $\le0.05$. Two choices changed after pre-registration and are disclosed here. The supremum statistic did not converge under the quadruple budget at $C{=}1000$ and disagreed with the 99.9th percentile of the same defects in both directions with $p<0.01$ (Table~\ref{tab:b21-2x2}); the distributional statistic is therefore the reading of record and the supremum is reported in full in the appendix. The Gaussian-coefficient null does not reproduce the sample spectrum it claims to match when $n$ is small relative to $d$ and inflates excesses for low-rank clouds, whereas the Haar construction does reproduce it (Table~\ref{tab:b27-nullvariants}); the Haar null is the null of record and both are reported. Rank-based calibration is what gives permutation scores their finite-sample validity \citep[\S5.1]{groger2026aristotelian}; a $z$-score on a handful of replicates is not rank-based, so $z$ is kept only as a secondary summary in the appendix. The null is aggregation-aware in the sense of \citet[\S5.2]{groger2026aristotelian}: the same statistic, the 99.9th percentile of the same $5{\times}10^5$ sampled defects per seed, is evaluated on every null replicate, so the max-statistic inflation is calibrated rather than ignored; the real cloud averages 10 quadruple seeds and each replicate 5 (3 in the text census), which affects variance, not bias. A negative excess is called \emph{beyond-null (clustered) structure}; ``tree-like'' is reserved for the depth test of \S\ref{sec:form}; ``genuine'' means BH-corrected $p\le0.05$ against the Haar spectrum-matched null, one hypothesis of ``no hierarchy'' (each null's scope: Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}). Sign-positive excess means \emph{less} tree-like than chance given the spectrum.

\paragraph{Projection and tasks.} A parameter-free map $\phi$ centers features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$ and preserves directions, so Euclidean, Poincar\'{e} and cosine readouts can be compared on the intact representation; two prototype tasks, nearest-centroid and 5-way 5-shot, score them for \S\ref{sec:practice} (Appendix~\ref{app:corollary}). % exp1, exp2

\paragraph{Sample-level readings sit within null noise in most cells.} The premise is read on per-image features, so we read it there first: on $\approx$1000 stratified training images per cell (CIFAR-100 and DTD, all 12 backbones) the raw supremum $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ lies in the low band the literature reports (0.087--0.145), and the calibrated excess is not genuine in 14 of 24 cells and genuine in 10 (DINOv2-S/B/L/G and SigLIP-B on CIFAR-100; ViT-S/B/L and DINOv2-L/G on DTD) (Table~\ref{tab:census}, last two columns; Appendix~\ref{app:sample}). This is the reading that motivates hyperbolic methods. Calibrated, it is absent in most supervised and contrastive cells and small where it survives: a low raw $\delta$ on image features is, to within $0.023$, what a random cloud of that dimension and spectrum produces. % expR62_samplelevel_record.csv

\paragraph{Class-level structure beyond the null is present in most models and every family.} Table~\ref{tab:census} summarizes the census of record on class centroids. Sign: 70 of 72 cells have negative excess. Genuine (BH-corrected $p\le0.05$): 49 of 72 overall and 18 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100; every family contributes genuine cells, and the smallest backbone, ViT-T, is genuine only on DTD. The other non-genuine ImageNet and CIFAR-100 cells are CLIP-B and SigLIP-B on ImageNet and ViT-S/B on CIFAR-100. Resampling the 100 images behind each ImageNet centroid moves the excess by at most $0.001$ (Table~\ref{tab:b36-bootstrap}). The flat datasets are sign-consistent but sit near the resolution limit at $C{=}10$, and a class-count control, random against WordNet-coherent subsets, shows that the reading tracks hierarchy rather than $C$ (Appendix~\ref{app:csweep}). Two ResNet-50 self-supervised models agree (Table~\ref{tab:b24-extra}), so the structure is not a ViT artifact. % expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR59_imagenet_bootstrap_summary.csv, expR45_convnet_rows.csv, expR60_c_sweep_record.csv

\paragraph{The excess certifies clustering, not depth.} The spectrum excess measures structure beyond second moments, and a pure star already has such structure: a synthetic 30-cluster star with no hierarchy scores $\approx-0.11$, as deep as any real cell (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). The excess measures how classes are arranged, not the geometry they are embedded in; whether the arrangement is deeper than a star is the question of the next paragraph, and \emph{whose} tree it is is the subject of \S\ref{sec:content}. % expR42_star_calibration.csv

\paragraph{Depth above the labelled clusters: certified on ImageNet, not on CIFAR-100.} A matched star tests hierarchy \emph{above} the labelled superclass clusters: Gaussian hubs with the real hub radius and, within each cluster, a Haar sample with the cluster's own covariance (10 star seeds), compared with the real centroids under the hub-randomizing null, which keeps every frame cluster intact, so the test certifies hierarchy above the frame only (Table~\ref{tab:b34-depthvariants}). With the frame at the finest clusters, our pre-set bar (power $\ge0.8$ for two- and three-level hierarchies at noise ratios $\le0.3$ for both $n$, false alarms $\le5$\% each way) fails at $n{=}100$, where pure stars with $K\ge12$ leaf clusters (under ten points per cluster) are declared more hierarchical in 17\% of runs, and is met at $n{=}1000$: power 1.00 at ratios $\le0.3$ and 0.90 at $0.6$, no false alarm either way (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}b; Table~\ref{tab:b35-power}). We certify the test within that regime only, a scoping adopted after the sweep. On ImageNet ($n{=}1000$, WordNet $K{=}30$) the real cloud is more hierarchical than its matched star beyond the noise in 4 of 12 backbones, ViT-S/B/L and DINOv2-L ($z$ from $-2.4$ to $-4.0$), not in the other eight, and never less (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}a); CIFAR-100 ($n{=}100$, $K{=}20$) lies in the unvalidated regime and its readings stay unvalidated (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). An earlier isotropic star, blind to each cluster's covariance, gave $z$ up to $+6.8$ on CIFAR-100 and 16\% false alarms on synthetic stars and is withdrawn; the hierarchically fine-tuned checkpoints of Appendix~\ref{app:interventions} were not stored, so that positive control could not be run. % expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR55b_depth_power_leafframe.csv, expR55_depth_power.csv

\paragraph{Imposing the geometry does not create depth.} MERU \citep{desai2023meru} embeds images and text on the Lorentz hyperboloid with an entailment objective, and its released Euclidean twin is a CLIP baseline with the same ViT, data and recipe minus the hyperbolic lift, so the pair isolates the geometry. Under the census of record the two show the same clustering excess on ImageNet images (MERU $-0.007$ to $-0.010$, CLIP $-0.006$ to $-0.010$ across ViT-S/B/L), and MERU's $\hat\delta_{99.9}$ in its own Lorentz metric differs from its Euclidean reading by at most $0.0002$. Under the validated depth test (ImageNet, $K{=}30$) neither MERU nor its twin is more hierarchical than a matched star at any size ($z$ from $-1.5$ to $-0.5$ for MERU, $-1.8$ to $-1.0$ for CLIP) (Table~\ref{tab:b30-meru}; the CIFAR-100 readings lie in the unvalidated regime). Training in hyperbolic space changes nothing at the class level: it neither creates the clustering nor deepens it. % expR63_meru_record.csv

\paragraph{Relation to neural collapse.} Neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} predicts that the class means of a supervised classifier converge to a simplex equiangular tight frame: equal norms, equal pairwise angles. An exact ETF is the extreme case of the star caveat: every four-point defect zero, $\delta=0$ with no hierarchy. Our reading differs in two ways. The arrangement we certify is not flat: class means cluster around superclass hubs, which an ETF would erase, and cutting the centroid dendrograms recovers the CIFAR-100 superclasses (\S\ref{sec:content}; curvature on the bridges in Appendix~\ref{app:orc}). And the same clustered arrangement appears beyond the null in models trained without class labels (DINO/DINOv2, CLIP/SigLIP), on transfer datasets and in causal LMs, outside the setting where neural collapse is stated; the depth test asks what neural collapse does not, whether there is hierarchy above the hubs, and answers yes for four ImageNet backbones, three of them supervised ViTs. % night/orc_bridges.csv

\paragraph{Scale and training.} Scale deepens the excess only for DINOv2: on CIFAR-10 from $-0.071$ (S) to $-0.130$ (G) and weakly on CIFAR-100 ($-0.031$ to $-0.040$), with no general trend elsewhere (Figure~\ref{fig:excess}b). Training moves the raw reading within architecture: non-hierarchical fine-tuning raises $\delta$ by 19--91\% and $\delta$ falls across transformer depth, while shuffling the embedding-to-label correspondence leaves it unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}). % expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, analysis4_finetuning.csv, e1_delta_by_layer.csv


\section{Findings II: Whose Tree}
\label{sec:content}

If the measured trees were a shared representation of reality, models should agree with each other and with human taxonomy; whether they appear to depends on how the trees are measured.

\paragraph{The naive map manufactures an island.} We extract each model's hierarchy explicitly, a dendrogram over the shared centroids, and compare hierarchies rather than representations: by ARI between cut partitions (adjusted Rand index, chance-corrected partition agreement) and by the cophenetic correlation (CPCC) between tree and distances. Under the naive configuration, Euclidean distances with average linkage, the map (Figure~\ref{fig:treemap}) is spectacular: supervised ViTs and contrastive VLMs form one block (cross-family ARI up to $0.82$) while the label-free DINOv2 family appears as an island (ARI $\le 0.06$ against every supervised model). The island's cuts are degenerate: DINOv2's Euclidean average-linkage dendrograms chain (up to $97\%$ of classes in one cluster), forcing ARI $\approx 0$ by construction. A configuration must therefore be selected by a criterion fixed \emph{without reference to the agreements being compared}; cophenetic fidelity alone re-selects the chaining configuration, so we fix \emph{highest CPCC among configurations whose reference cuts are non-degenerate for every model} (largest cluster $\le 50\%$), which selects cosine-average on ImageNet and cosine-complete on CIFAR-100; across admissible degeneracy bounds the ImageNet gap ranges from $0.13$ to $0.38$, never back to the categorical island (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). % exp22_tree_similarity_{imagenet,cifar100}.npz, exp23_treemap_controls.npz, exp23_config_diagnostics.json

\paragraph{The island is an artifact; sharing is graded.} Three measures give the same reading under the selected configurations (Figures~\ref{fig:treemap}b and \ref{fig:treemapc100}b). At the cut, the island shrinks from categorical to moderate (ImageNet ARI $0.38$ vs block $0.48$). In cophenetic correlation a gap remains ($0.48$ vs $0.78$). In triplet agreement, a cut-free measure, the gap closes entirely under angular configurations ($0.77$ vs $0.76$), whereas it persists under Euclidean ones without a cut ($0.47$ vs $0.74$; cophenetic $0.36$ vs $0.80$; Table~\ref{tab:b33-cutfree}). Across every non-degenerate configuration label-free models recover the human taxonomy partially and supervision deepens the resemblance: paired within each admissible configuration, the label-supervised ViT recovers more than each DINOv2 model in 11 of 12 comparisons, and DINOv2 recovers the 20 CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI $0.2$--$0.5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). The gap is a property of the measure as much as of the models. % exp23_treemap_controls.npz, expR58_treemap_cutfree_summary.csv

\paragraph{The self-supervised tree lives in angles.} DINOv2's semantics concentrate in the angular component: sibling-triplet agreement is $0.91$ under cosine against $0.71$ under Euclidean distance for DINOv2-L (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}), which is why its Euclidean dendrograms chain into the naive island and why the triplet gap closes only under angular configurations. Where the structure lives decides which readout collects it (\S\ref{sec:practice}). % exp10_local_vs_global.csv, expR57_census_cosine_haar_p999_200.csv

\paragraph{WordNet alignment follows supervision, not tree-likeness.} Spearman correlation between inter-centroid distances and WordNet tree distances on ImageNet: $+0.57$ to $+0.59$ for CLIP/SigLIP, $+0.49$ to $+0.53$ for supervised ViTs, $+0.36$ for DINO-B, and only $+0.18$ to $+0.22$ for DINOv2, genuine on ImageNet and both CIFAR sets under the record, with shuffle and Gaussian controls at $\approx0$ (Table~\ref{tab:census}). Cutting centroid dendrograms recovers the 20 true CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI up to $0.61$ (permuted labels $\approx0$). Across the three families, WordNet content and robustness of the excess anti-correlate: supervised and contrastive are the most aligned and the most fragile, DINOv2 the least aligned and the most robust. % exp3_alignment.csv, exp8_p1_recovery.csv

\paragraph{The recovery is not WordNet circularity.} ImageNet classes are WordNet synsets, so recovering WordNet there could be circular. Three controls bound the circularity: WordNet-coherent groupings have lower raw $\delta$ than random ones in 11/12 models, single-image distances still correlate with WordNet ($\rho$ up to $+0.26$), and the decoupling above is the opposite of label injection. Repeating the tree map on DBpedia Classes, a three-level ontology independent of WordNet (219 leaf classes; the three text embedders), closes the door: no configuration degenerates, the embedders recover the true 70-way level-2 partition at ARI $0.21$--$0.29$ in all six configurations and agree with each other at cross-model ARI $0.65$--$0.82$. The recovered hierarchy is real, shared and configuration-robust; the fragility above is a property of the (family, concept set) pair. % exp1, exp8_p6_pooling.csv, exp27_dbpedia_treemap.json

\paragraph{Text: form depends on recipe, scale and probe.} On DBpedia, a real three-level text hierarchy, the sentence embedders show beyond-null excess under the record ($-0.017$ to $-0.021$, every replicate above the real value; Table~\ref{tab:a9}); on the harder probe, the 1000 ImageNet class names, the same embedders are not genuine (excess $+0.002$ to $+0.004$) despite raw values indistinguishable from vision backbones whose excess is: presence of structure is a property of the (model, concept set) pair. Within causal LMs on the class-name probe, GPT-2 L and XL are genuine under all four null$\times$statistic constructions and under cosine, whereas S and M flip across readings (S genuine under the record, $p=0.02$; M not, $p=0.15$): robust at the larger scales, fragile at the smaller. Pythia is genuine at every scale tested, and so is OLMo-1B; extraction protocol and prompt template move the fragile values but not the scale ordering (Appendix~\ref{app:textcensus}). % expR53_text_haar_p999_200.csv, expR53_text_haar_sup_200.csv, expR53_text_gauss_p999_200.csv, expR48b_text_census200_bs1.csv, expR57_text_cosine_haar_p999_200.csv, expR61_dbpedia_record.csv

\paragraph{Local agreement survives calibration.} We apply the permutation calibration of \citet{groger2026aristotelian}: for each of the 66 model pairs, $K{=}200$ permutations of the class correspondence give the null distribution, the threshold $\tau_{0.05}$ is its $\lceil 0.95(K{+}1)\rceil$-th order statistic, and the calibrated score is $\max\{(s-\tau_{0.05})/(1-\tau_{0.05}),0\}$. Mutual-kNN agreement survives ($0.472$ raw, $0.464$ calibrated, every pair at $p=1/201$, null mean $0.010$ = chance $10/999$), and so does centroid CKA ($0.633$ raw, $0.577$ calibrated, null mean $0.108$); the Poincar\'{e} reading improves neither (kNN $0.472\to0.425$, CKA $0.633\to0.640$; Table~\ref{tab:b31-groger}). % exp21b_local_global_K200.csv

\section{Consequences for Imposing Curvature}
\label{sec:practice}

\paragraph{Raw $\delta$ cannot select a curvature.} \citet{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR} set the curvature of the Poincar\'{e} ball from the measured relative hyperbolicity $\delta_{\text{rel}}=2\delta/\mathrm{diam}$ as $c=(0.144/\delta_{\text{rel}})^2$, with $0.144$ the effective $\delta_{\text{rel}}$ of the ball at their clipping radius, which gave $c\approx0.33$ on their datasets. Since raw $\delta$ is dominated by dimension and spectrum (\S\ref{sec:instrument}--\S\ref{sec:form}), a curvature chosen this way tracks the backbone's width rather than its hierarchy: the iid Gaussian clouds of Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, which have no structure at all, would receive $c$ from $0.48$ at $d{=}192$ to $2.5$ at $d{=}1536$. The calibrated excess is the quantity to read, and it is small: $-0.005$ to $-0.020$ on ImageNet centroids, and within noise or at most $-0.023$ on image features. % exp1_delta_controls.csv, expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR62_samplelevel_record.csv

\paragraph{Measure before imposing.} For a frozen backbone the instrument answers three questions in minutes: whether the class geometry has structure beyond a spectrum-matched null (the excess and its rank), whether that structure is deeper than a star (the matched-star test, in the $n\approx1000$ regime where it is validated), and where it lives (angular or radial, from the cosine reading of \S\ref{sec:content}). None of the three is readable from raw $\delta$, and in this census the answers are: usually yes, rarely, and, for the self-supervised family, in angles.

\paragraph{Which zero-cost metric collects it.} Changing the readout metric on the intact representation costs nothing and collects what is there. Cosine collects the self-supervised structure; the Poincar\'{e} readout adds $+0.1$ to $+1.3$ pp over cosine for the contrastive VLMs on prototype tasks and is inconsistent for the others ($-1.7$ to $+1.1$ pp); on flat-label datasets no metric helps; sample-level tasks never benefit. The full study, its confidence intervals and its negatives are in Appendix~\ref{app:corollary}. % exp2_metric_controls.csv, exp2b_normalized_stack.csv, exp24_val_metric_selection.csv

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the paper establishes.} Read correctly, foundation models organize classes into clustered structure that is occasionally hierarchical, moderately shared and supervision-dependent in content; they do not converge to one common tree, and their raw tree-likeness is not evidence for hyperbolic geometry. In three acts: the premise of latent hyperbolicity is confounded, and its sample-level reading sits within null noise in most cells and is small elsewhere (\S\ref{sec:instrument}--\S\ref{sec:form}); what survives at the class level is clustered structure in most models (49 of 72 cells), hierarchy above the superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones, no depth from imposing the geometry, and a moderately shared, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy whose self-supervised part lives in angles (\S\ref{sec:form}--\S\ref{sec:content}); and what follows is that curvature should be measured, not assumed, and that a zero-cost readout collects what is there (\S\ref{sec:practice}).

\paragraph{Open questions.} Why the structure is clustered but rarely deep is open: the superclass hubs are recovered by every recipe, yet only four backbones place them hierarchically beyond a star, and the interventions of Appendix~\ref{app:interventions} move raw $\delta$ without a stored point cloud to test depth. Why the self-supervised tree is angular, when the supervised and contrastive trees are not, is the second question; the objective's invariances are the natural suspect and \S\ref{sec:practice} reads it only as a fact about readouts.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~The spectrum excess certifies structure beyond second moments, star-like clustering included; the matched-star test certifies hierarchy above its frame only and is validated for $n\approx1000$ (not below about ten points per cluster), so depth is certified on ImageNet alone; hierarchy within the labelled clusters is not tested (Appendix~\ref{app:star}), and the hierarchy's content rests on \S\ref{sec:content}. (ii)~The census is class-centroid geometry on six datasets; other concept sets may expose different structure. (iii)~Causal-LM values (not the scale ordering) are template-conditioned. (iv)~The objective--geometry link is correlational (triplet-bounded, not closed); the within-dataset $\delta$--gain prediction rests chiefly on DINOv2's scale range (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). (v)~Downstream scope is prototype-based metric tasks with modest gains. (vi)~The parallelogram descriptor $\xi$ tracks norm dispersion rather than curvature (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}) and is reported only as such. (vii)~A causal LM's class representation depends on the extraction protocol as much as on the model: left-padded batching moves $\hat\delta$ by up to $0.018$, of the order of the L/XL excess. (viii)~The sample-level reading covers two datasets and one image budget.

\subsection{The corollary: diagnosing exploitability}
\label{app:corollary}
\paragraph{One parameter-free projection and two tasks serve the corollary.} The parameter-free map $\phi(x)=\tanh(\tfrac12\|s(x-\mu)\|)\cdot\frac{s(x-\mu)}{\|s(x-\mu)\|}$ centers features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$, and preserves directions; prototypes in the ball are means of projected points re-mapped through the origin. Tasks: nearest-centroid (NC) and 5-way 5-shot (FS, 1000 paired episodes); metric arms: Euclidean (R), Poincar\'{e} (H), the radial map with Euclidean distances (RT), cosine (COS), and the L2-normalized stack. Changing the readout metric on the intact configuration, which is what the rule selects among, is distinct from deforming the configuration itself. % exp1, exp2

\paragraph{$\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts the gain within datasets.} Within each dataset the Poincar\'{e} advantage tracks $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ (NC $r=-0.83$ on ImageNet) and survives partialing out dimension, baseline accuracy, family and spectral covariates; without DINOv2 it holds on ImageNet and CIFAR-100 but not on CIFAR-10/DTD (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). Pooled cross-dataset correlations are family-driven and not used as evidence; on flat-label datasets no metric helps. Gains carry paired confidence intervals and a McNemar test (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). % table1_regenerated, exp20, night/correlation_cis, exp13

\paragraph{The training objective picks the metric.} The radial map with Euclidean distances changes nothing, so the gain is the metric itself, and the split follows the objective: cosine suffices for DINOv2, Poincar\'{e} $\approx$ cosine for supervised ViTs, and Poincar\'{e} beats cosine for CLIP by up to $+1.3$pp even after L2 normalization (Figure~\ref{fig:bestmetric} in Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}; held-out selection and policy comparison in this appendix). The rule is read off the families it is scored on: it describes them rather than validating a policy. % exp2, exp2b, exp24

\paragraph{A diagnostic, not a policy.} Cosine is a safe default, gating on $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is the weakest policy tested (this appendix), and gains are modest and limited to prototype-based tasks (sample-level tasks never benefit, Appendix~\ref{app:sample}). On DBpedia, a three-level text hierarchy, the diagnostic predicts correctly on both sides: genuine excess, no metric gain. % exp2, exp20, exp24, exp14, exp15

\subsection{Parallelogram defect $\xi$: a norm-structure descriptor}
\label{app:xi}
\paragraph{$\xi$ reads norm structure, not curvature.} A parallelogram estimator $\xi$, intended as a curvature sign, is driven negative by heterogeneous norms alone, so it is reported only as a norm-structure descriptor. % exp12, expR43_xi_norm_references.csv
For a triangle $(a,b,c)$ with $m$ the approximate midpoint of $(b,c)$ (the minimizer of $D(\cdot,b){+}D(\cdot,c){+}|D(\cdot,b){-}D(\cdot,c)|$), the parallelogram defect is $\xi = \big(D(a,m)^2 + \tfrac14 D(b,c)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,b)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,c)^2\big)\,/\,(2\,D(a,m)\,\mathrm{diam})$, averaged over $2{\times}10^4$ sampled triangles: $0$ in Euclidean space, positive on spheres, negative in hyperbolic space and on trees.
