\begin{abstract}
Foundation models are often said to converge toward shared representations, a claim recent calibrated analyses qualify. We ask the same question of geometry: what structure of a model's inter-class representation survives calibrated measurement, and is it shared across models? We measure tree-likeness (Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity, Ollivier--Ricci curvature) on frozen models' class representations, calibrating the estimator on reference geometries and reading every value as an excess over a null matched in dimension and covariance spectrum. Three findings emerge. (1)~Genuine tree-like \emph{form} is widespread but not universal: it survives the matched null in 69 of 72 vision model$\times$dataset cells, an excess that a synthetic calibration shows to certify clustered, star-like structure beyond second moments, with hierarchy depth as a measurable modulation on top; in text it depends on training recipe and scale, emerging with size in GPT-2 and OLMo and absent in classic sentence embedders whose raw $\delta$ equals that of vision-contrastive models. Where present, the form is learned. (2)~Its \emph{content} is substantially shared, but comparing trees needs calibration too: a naive dendrogram comparison isolates the label-free family as an island, a chaining artifact; once controlled, all recipes recover the human taxonomy and supervision deepens the resemblance. (3)~As a corollary, the descriptors diagnose where leaving Euclidean distances pays on prototype-based tasks, and the training objective selects which zero-cost metric collects the gain. The answer to the title question is a graded yes: the shared tree is the human taxonomy, visible only through a calibrated instrument and relative to a concept set.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Semantic knowledge is hierarchical: instances belong to categories (a husky is a dog), categories to superordinate classes (dogs are mammals), and so on upward. The natural geometric home for such hierarchies is hyperbolic space \citep{nickel2017poincare, sarkar2011low}, and a long line of work \emph{imposes} it at training time \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, chen2022fully, desai2023meru}. A complementary observation, latent hyperbolicity, holds that standard Euclidean models already organize concepts in a tree-like metric structure \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, yang2025hyperbolic}. Is that structure hierarchical, and for whom?

The na\"ive measurement of this question is confounded. Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity \citep{Gromov1987} assigns $0$ to trees, but three artifacts push raw readings down without any hierarchy: high-dimensional clouds concentrate toward equidistance (a degenerate star tree), anisotropic spectra mimic low-dimensional behavior, and small class counts shrink the quadruple population. These artifacts are real: GPT-2-S sits \emph{above} its matched null, and classic sentence embedders share raw $\delta$ with vision-contrastive models while carrying none of their genuine structure. % exp18_text_nulls.csv

Our first contribution is therefore an \textbf{instrument}: the exact estimator calibrated on reference geometries (trees, hyperbolic regions, spheres, Gaussians of growing dimension), every reading expressed as an \emph{excess} over a null matched in dimension and covariance spectrum with a per-cell $z$-score, a parallelogram descriptor of norm structure, and a class-subsampling control that separates hierarchy depth from class count. With the instrument in hand, we ask the question that recent convergence work \citep{huh2024platonic, groger2026aristotelian} asks of similarity, now of geometry: \emph{what survives calibration, and is it shared across models? Is there a Platonic tree?}

The answer has two halves, which the title condenses. \textbf{Form is widespread, and where it exists it is genuine and learned:} 69 of 72 vision model$\times$dataset combinations sit below their matched null, the excess deepens with scale within each transfer dataset, and four ablations tie the structure to learning. % exp20_null_ztable.csv, exp12, fig3_causal
In text, the same instrument reveals heterogeneity that raw $\delta$ hides: genuine form emerges with scale in causal LMs and is absent in classic sentence embedders on the class-name probe yet present for the same embedders on a real text hierarchy: form is a property of the (model, concept set) pair. % exp18, exp14
\textbf{The tree is substantially shared, and tree comparison needs calibration too:} a naive tree-to-tree comparison isolates the label-free family as a categorical island that controls expose as a chaining artifact; once controlled, label-free models recover the human taxonomy substantially, supervision deepens the resemblance, a residual gap persists on ImageNet, and hyperbolic distances buy no cross-model agreement. The descriptors also diagnose where zero-cost non-Euclidean metrics pay, with statistics reported cautiously.

\textbf{Our contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A calibrated instrument for tree-likeness}: estimator calibration, spectrum-matched nulls with per-cell $z$-scores, a synthetic calibration of what they certify, and a class-count control, together with the demonstration that raw $\delta$ readings mislead (identical raw values, opposite null verdicts).
\item \textbf{A calibrated census}: in vision, genuine tree form in 69/72 model$\times$dataset cells, scale-deepening on the excess basis, and learned; in text, form that depends on recipe and scale.
\item \textbf{The map of trees, with its own calibration lesson}: a tree-to-tree comparison across models whose naive version manufactures an island (metric--linkage chaining) and whose controlled version shows graded sharing of the human taxonomy across all training recipes, with a relational base genuinely shared and no cross-model agreement bought by hyperbolic reading.
\item \textbf{A scoped, training-free corollary}: the calibrated descriptors \emph{diagnose} exploitability, predicting where the sign of the non-Euclidean gain falls on prototype-based tasks, while label flatness and the training objective make the operational choice, cosine being the safe default; validated across tasks and a text hierarchy, negatives stated.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperbolic deep learning: imposing vs measuring.} Building on tree embeddability \citep{sarkar2011low} and Poincar\'{e} embeddings \citep{nickel2017poincare, ganea2018hyperbolic}, hyperbolic methods span vision \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, ermolov2022hyperbolic, atigh2022hyperbolic, bdeir2024fully}, vision--language \citep{desai2023meru, pal2025compositional}, graphs \citep{chami2019hyperbolic}, and language \citep{chen2022fully, he2026helm, sinha2024learning}. Latent hyperbolicity, low measured $\delta$ in standard models, has been reported for CNN/ViT features \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully}, token embeddings \citep{yang2025hyperbolic}, and word vectors \citep{tifrea2019poincare}. Those readings are sample-level and uncalibrated; transplanting our instrument to sample-level features (Appendix~\ref{app:sample}) finds raw $\delta$ in the same low band but excesses mostly within null noise: the calibration lesson applies there too, and our census is about inter-class (centroid) geometry.

\paragraph{Geometric analyses of representations.} Representation similarity \citep{kornblith2019cka}, intrinsic dimension \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic}, and neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} characterize Euclidean structure without testing tree-likeness. Estimating Gromov hyperbolicity on sampled quadruples has known bias and scale caveats \citep{fournier2015computing}, which our calibration-plus-null design addresses operationally.

\paragraph{Representational convergence.} The Platonic Representation Hypothesis \citep{huh2024platonic} argues that models converge toward shared representations; \citet{koepke2026cave} stress-test cross-modal convergence at scale, and \citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that permutation-calibrated similarity removes most apparent global convergence while local neighborhood structure survives. We are the geometric complement of this program: the same calibration philosophy applied within models and, in \S\ref{sec:content}, across them.

\paragraph{Background.} For four points $w,x,y,z$ of a metric space, sort the three pairing sums $d(w,x){+}d(y,z)$, $d(w,y){+}d(x,z)$, $d(w,z){+}d(x,y)$ as $S_{\max}\ge S_{\mathrm{mid}}\ge S_{\min}$; the four-point defect is $(S_{\max}-S_{\mathrm{mid}})/2$ and $\delta$ is its supremum. Trees have $\delta=0$; under this four-point definition the hyperbolic plane has $\delta=\ln 2\approx0.693$ \citep{nica2016strong}, distinct from the thin-triangles constant $\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.881$. We use the normalized $\delta_{\text{norm}}=\delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ throughout: on this scale hyperbolic regions drift to $0$ as they grow while spheres stay high, so the intuitive ordering trees $<$ hyperbolic $<$ spherical holds. Ollivier--Ricci curvature \citep{ollivier2009ricci} on a kNN graph is our local companion: positive on tightly clustered neighborhoods, negative on tree-like bridges.

\section{The Instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}

This section builds the measurement layer in whose units every later claim is stated: raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is interpretable only against calibrated references and matched nulls.

\paragraph{Class representations.} Each frozen model is a fixed feature extractor; classes are represented by centroids (vision: the mean of each class's cached training embeddings, $100$ on ImageNet, the full cached split of $80$--$6{,}000$ elsewhere; text: embeddings of ``a photo of a \{class\}'' over the 1000 ImageNet classes, plus the corpus variations of \S\ref{sec:form}). The calibrated census covers 12 vision backbones $\times$ 6 datasets (a \emph{cell} is one model$\times$dataset pair) and 16 text models (panel in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}); the task grid covers the 10 vision backbones with cached per-dataset features (all but DINO-B and SigLIP-B).
Estimator settings: $5{\times}10^5$ quadruples per seed, 10 seeds (exact enumeration when $C\le 30$); ORC on $k{=}10$ kNN graphs (ranking invariant for $k\in\{5,\dots,50\}$). On the transfer sets the excess is budget-stable from $10^5$ quadruples and image-resampling moves it far less than its size; on ImageNet every sign holds at every budget but magnitudes drift by up to $0.02$; a 99.9th-percentile variant preserves every sign (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{Calibration.} On reference geometries (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}) the estimator recovers the tree zero and the $\ln 2$ four-point constant of $\mathbb{H}^2$ ($\delta_{\text{norm}}\to0$ as hyperbolic regions grow), spheres stay high ($0.14$--$0.18$), and iid Gaussians drift low as $d$ grows ($0.104\to0.046$ from $d{=}192$ to $1536$): the dimension confound. A low raw value is not evidence.

\paragraph{Matched nulls and the excess.} For every (model, class set) cell we synthesize null clouds with the same $n$, $d$, mean and covariance \emph{spectrum} (Gaussian coefficients recombined with the real singular values; a PC-permutation null confirms the readings, while an exact-sample-spectrum construction gives smaller excesses for low-rank clouds on the small-$C$ sets, Appendix~\ref{app:robust}), and report the \textbf{excess} $\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{real}}-\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{null}}$ with a $z$-score against the combined null-replicate and estimator noise (5 null replicates for $\delta$, 20 for $\xi$); $|z|$ beyond a few units means ``below every replicate'', not a Gaussian tail; a full 20-replicate re-run reproduces every sign of the census (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). ``Tree-like'' below always means \emph{negative excess}, and ``genuine'' means \emph{beyond the spectrum-matched Gaussian null}, one hypothesis of ``no hierarchy'' (each null's scope: Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}). Sign-positive excess means \emph{less} tree-like than chance given the spectrum. Whitening is not a usable alternative (it collapses centroids toward a regular simplex, itself a degenerate star).

\paragraph{A norm-structure descriptor.} A parallelogram estimator $\xi$ (midpoint defect of triangles) was intended as a curvature sign, but references with heterogeneous norms and no curvature drive it negative (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}); we therefore report it only as a descriptor of norm structure, never as curvature. % exp12, expR43_xi_norm_references.csv

\paragraph{Class-count control.} Because $C$ sets the quadruple population, we sweep ImageNet subsets $C\in\{10,\dots,1000\}$ with two compositions: random (spanning the full hierarchy) and WordNet-coherent (siblings, effectively \emph{flat}). Results in \S\ref{sec:form}; the control makes ``needs a real hierarchy'' a measured variable.

\paragraph{Projection and tasks (for the corollary).} The parameter-free map $\phi(x)=\tanh(\tfrac12\|s(x-\mu)\|)\cdot\frac{s(x-\mu)}{\|s(x-\mu)\|}$ centers features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$, and preserves directions; prototypes in the ball are means of projected points re-mapped through the origin. Tasks: nearest-centroid (NC) and 5-way 5-shot (FS, 1000 paired episodes); metric arms: Euclidean (R), Poincar\'{e} (H), the radial map with Euclidean distances (RT), cosine (COS), and the L2-normalized stack. Deforming the configuration (sphericizing raises $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ for all 12 models) is distinct from changing the readout metric on the intact configuration, which is what the rule selects among. % exp1, exp2

\paragraph{What the excess certifies.} A star is a tree, so clustered structure alone lowers raw $\delta$: on a synthetic 30-cluster star with no hierarchy (Gaussian clusters around Gaussian centers) the excess over the spectrum null is $\approx-0.11$, as deep as any real cell, and a null that keeps every cluster intact and randomizes only the hub arrangement cannot repair this, because a star already minimizes $\delta$ and any hub randomization can only raise it (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). The spectrum excess therefore certifies \emph{structure beyond second moments}, clustering included; what it does not certify is depth. Depth is measured separately by the class-subsampling control below (a hierarchy-spanning class set is more tree-like than a flat one at equal $C$), and \emph{whose} tree it is by \S\ref{sec:content}. % expR42_star_calibration.csv

\paragraph{Text: the instrument reveals what raw $\delta$ hides.} Within causal LMs (Figure~\ref{fig:textnulls}), genuine form \emph{emerges with scale}: monotonically in GPT-2 ($+0.048 \to -0.013 \to -0.034 \to -0.048$, S$\to$XL) and in OLMo (at null at 1B, genuine at 7B); Pythia carries it at every scale ($-0.035$..$-0.041$). The rising nulls track the effective rank of the centroid covariance (GPT-2: $1.3\to56$), and genuineness is not a proxy for anisotropy (GTE-base has the highest rank, $98$, and sits at its null). A PC-permutation null confirms every verdict, with one nuance: GPT-2-S's positive excess is specific to the Gaussian spectrum null (PC-permutation $z=-1.4$, n.s.), so S carries no genuine form under either null while M/L/XL are genuinely negative under both ($z=-3.7/-7.8/-6.4$). % exp18b_text_anisotropy.csv Classic sentence embedders (BGE/GTE/E5, base and large) sit at their nulls on this probe ($+0.001$ to $+0.011$) despite raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ values ($0.114$--$0.124$) indistinguishable from vision-contrastive models whose excess is genuine: \emph{identical raw values, opposite verdicts}, the cleanest demonstration that the uncalibrated scale is not interpretable. The only LLM-backboned embedder tested (GTE-Qwen2-1.5B) is genuine ($-0.019$), patterning with the causal-LM scale story rather than with its embedder training recipe. % exp18_text_nulls.csv
Two scopes. First, causal-LM values are template-sensitive (range up to $.065$; embedders $\le.012$), but the scale trend is not: with nulls on ten templates, S is genuine on none (\emph{above} its null on 7), M on 3, L and XL on 9/10 each, including a definition-only template with no class names (Appendix~\ref{app:templates}). % expR30_template_nulls.csv
Second, the same embedders that sit at null on class-name prompts show genuine excess ($-0.020$ to $-0.028$) on a real three-level text hierarchy (DBpedia): presence of form is a property of the (model, concept set) pair. % exp4+exp17, exp14

\section{Findings II: Shared Content, Fragile Measurement}
\label{sec:content}

If the measured trees were a shared representation of reality, models should agree with each other and with human taxonomy. Whether they appear to depends on how the trees are measured: the naive-vs-calibrated gap is the paper's thesis in miniature.

\paragraph{A naive map of trees, and its artifact.} We extract each model's hierarchy explicitly (a dendrogram over the shared centroids) and compare hierarchies, not representations, by ARI between cut partitions and cophenetic correlation. Under the naive configuration, Euclidean distances with average linkage, the map (Figure~\ref{fig:treemap}) is spectacular: supervised ViTs and contrastive VLMs form one block (cross-family ARI up to $0.82$), while the label-free DINOv2 family appears as an island (ARI $\le 0.06$ against every supervised model). % exp22_tree_similarity_{imagenet,cifar100}.npz
The categorical island, however, is a measurement artifact. Its cuts are degenerate: DINOv2's Euclidean average-linkage dendrograms chain (up to $97\%$ of classes in one cluster), forcing ARI $\approx 0$ by construction, and Euclidean distance is the metric our diagnostics flag as mismatched for this family (\S\ref{sec:form}). % exp23_treemap_controls.npz
A configuration must be selected by a criterion fixed \emph{without reference to the agreements being compared}; cophenetic fidelity alone fails (it favors average linkage and re-selects the chaining configuration), so we fix: \emph{highest mean cophenetic fidelity among configurations whose reference cuts are non-degenerate for every model} (largest cluster $\le 50\%$). Both quantities are tabulated per configuration in Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}; the criterion selects cosine-average on ImageNet and cosine-complete on CIFAR-100. The $0.5$ degeneracy bound is the criterion's one free parameter; sensitivity in Appendix~\ref{app:treemap} (conclusions unchanged for every bound). % threshold sensitivity, exp23_config_diagnostics.json
Under the selected configurations (Figure~\ref{fig:treemap}b,d) the island shrinks from categorical to moderate (ImageNet $0.38$ vs block $0.48$; CIFAR-100 $0.39$ vs block $0.63$), and across \emph{all} non-degenerate configurations DINOv2 recovers the true 20 superclasses at ARI $0.2$--$0.5$ (vs $0.003$--$0.12$ in the naive configuration), reaching the supervised band ($0.48$--$0.52$ vs $0.54$--$0.60$) only under cosine-Ward, the configuration where the CIFAR-100 island also closes fully ($0.56$ vs block-internal $0.61$; cophenetic $0.74$ vs $0.74$). % exp23, per-config ground-truth checks

\paragraph{The corrected picture.} Three statements hold across every non-degenerate configuration: the categorical island is an artifact; label-free models recover the human taxonomy substantially (from $\approx 0$ to $0.2$--$0.5$), with supervision deepening the resemblance: paired within each admissible configuration, the label-supervised ViT recovers more than each DINOv2 model in 11 of 12 comparisons (gaps $+0.03$ to $+0.36$; the exception is DINOv2-L under cosine-complete, $-0.02$); and tree-to-tree comparison itself is fragile to configuration. A moderate label-free gap persists on ImageNet under every configuration (WordNet correlation $0.13$--$0.24$ vs $0.46$--$0.57$), consistent with the same reading: a real tree, least aligned with WordNet at the top.

\paragraph{Whose tree?} Spearman correlation between inter-centroid distances and WordNet tree distances on ImageNet: $+0.57$ to $+0.59$ for CLIP/SigLIP, $+0.49$ to $+0.53$ for supervised ViTs, $+0.36$ for DINO-B, and only $+0.18$ to $+0.22$ for DINOv2, the most tree-like family, with shuffle and Gaussian controls at $\approx0$ (Table~\ref{tab:census}). % exp3_alignment.csv
Cutting centroid dendrograms recovers the 20 true CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI up to $0.61$ (four linkage methods; permuted labels $\approx0$). % exp8_p1_recovery.csv
On HierarCaps \citep{alper2024hierarcaps} (1000 four-level caption chains), text encoders show the same reading with no class set: the projection radius orders levels within a chain ($\rho$(level, radius) $+0.32$..$+0.87$ over six encoders; strictly monotone $10$--$54\%$ vs $4.2\%$ chance), sibling triplets hit $0.83$--$0.97$ (chance $0.5$), and the leaf excess repeats the text census (Table~\ref{tab:a8}). % exp16_hierarcaps.csv
Three controls bound circularity: WordNet-coherent groupings are more tree-like than random ones in 11/12 models; single-image distances still correlate with WordNet ($\rho$ up to $+0.26$); and the decoupling above is the opposite of label injection. % exp1, exp8_p6_pooling.csv
DINOv2's semantics concentrate in the angular component (sibling triplet agreement $0.91/0.85$ under cosine vs $0.71/0.63$ Euclidean for L/G), which is also why Euclidean dendrograms chain for this family and produce the naive island. The census ``exception'' dissolves here: real structure (Table~\ref{tab:b18-p999}), least aligned with WordNet's top level: the form-vs-content split. % exp10_local_vs_global.csv

\paragraph{A non-WordNet control.} ImageNet classes are WordNet synsets, so recovering WordNet there could be circular. Repeating the tree map on DBpedia Classes, a three-level ontology independent of WordNet (219 leaf classes; the three text embedders), closes this door: no configuration degenerates, the embedders recover the true 70-way level-2 partition at ARI $0.21$--$0.29$ across all six configurations, and they agree with each other at cross-model ARI $0.65$--$0.82$. The recovered non-WordNet hierarchy is real, shared, and configuration-robust; the fragility of \S\ref{sec:content} is a property of the (family, concept set) pair. % exp27_dbpedia_treemap.json

\paragraph{What is shared, calibrated.} Applying the permutation-calibration of \citet{groger2026aristotelian} to our cross-model measures: local neighborhood agreement survives calibration (mutual-kNN $0.474\to0.464$ calibrated over the 66 pairs; null $0.010$), and so does centroid CKA ($0.636\to0.529$): centroids distill a genuinely shared relational base. % exp21_local_global.csv
A separate statement concerns the metric: the Poincar\'{e} reading improves neither agreement (kNN $0.474 \to 0.427$; CKA $0.636 \to 0.644$). Hyperbolic distances buy no cross-model alignment under any of our measures — as expected, since $\phi$ preserves directions and rescales norms monotonically.

\section{Corollary: Diagnosing Exploitability}
\label{sec:corollary}

Is there non-Euclidean inter-class structure worth exploiting for a frozen backbone, and which zero-cost metric collects it? The descriptors diagnose the first; label flatness and the objective decide the second; negatives included.

\paragraph{Whether to leave Euclidean.} The NC advantage of the Poincar\'{e} projection correlates with $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ at $r=-0.45$ pooled (cluster-bootstrap CI95 $[-0.60,-0.36]$) and within datasets at $-0.83$ (ImageNet) and $-0.87$ (CIFAR-100; CIs in Appendix~\ref{app:tables}); the correlations survive partialing out dimension and baseline accuracy ($r=-0.62$/$-0.79$), so they are no proxy for backbone quality; partialing out effective rank and norm dispersion weakens but does not remove them within datasets ($-0.20$ to $-0.53$), while the pooled correlation does not survive ($-0.33\to-0.07$): part of raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is spectral; its residual is the within-dataset signal. Because the 10 backbones are three families with nested scales, we also demean both variables within family: the within-dataset correlations remain at $-0.31$ to $-0.73$ (NC) and $-0.63$ to $-0.86$ (FS), carried chiefly by DINOv2's scale range (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). % partial correlations computed from table1_regenerated + exp20 For FS the pooled correlation is not robust (CI $[-0.50,+0.12]$; 56/60 cells gain, compressing variance) and the claim is within-dataset only: $-0.67$ to $-0.78$ on CIFAR-100/10/DTD, all excluding zero. % night/correlation_cis.csv, table1_regenerated.csv
On flat datasets no metric helps and no correlation survives; headline effects carry paired per-episode CIs ($\pm0.03$--$0.20$pp) and a McNemar test on the largest NC gain (DINOv2-L CIFAR-100 $+2.05$pp: 242 vs 37 discordant, $p\approx4\times10^{-38}$). % exp13_mcnemar.csv

\paragraph{Which metric collects the gain.} The identical radial map with Euclidean distances changes little (mean $|\mathrm{RT{-}R}|=0.20$pp FS / $0.12$pp NC, no systematic sign), so the H$-$R gain is the metric. Against cosine the split follows the objective: cosine suffices for DINOv2 (whose semantics are angular), H $\approx$ cosine for supervised ViTs, and H beats cosine for contrastive CLIP by $+0.9$ to $+1.3$pp on transfer datasets even after L2 normalization (ImageNet $+0.1$ to $+0.2$pp; Table~\ref{tab:census}, last columns). % exp2_metric_controls.csv, exp2b
The advantage of the best zero-cost metric over Euclidean is itself predicted by $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ within each dataset (FS $r=-0.60$ to $-0.83$, and $-0.55$ to $-0.82$ after demeaning by family; Figure~\ref{fig:bestmetric}); the pooled correlations ($-0.31$ all cells, $-0.33$ hierarchical) do not survive the family control ($-0.18$/$-0.04$), so the pooled version is family-driven and the prediction is a within-dataset statement (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). Selecting the metric on a held-out half of the episodes retains the full advantage ($+0.63$ vs the oracle's $+0.64$pp over 60 cells; $+0.78$pp both on hierarchical cells), so it is no test-selection artifact. With no validation data, the objective rule beats always-cosine on FS ($+0.41$ vs $+0.28$pp on 40 hierarchical cells; $p=0.011$); practical ordering: validation pick if a split exists, else the objective rule, Euclidean on flat labels. % exp24_val_metric_selection.csv

\paragraph{What the diagnostic is, and is not.} Cosine alone already improves on Euclidean in most hierarchical cells, so the \emph{whether} question has a high base rate there; the descriptors' value is predicting \emph{where the sign falls} and flagging the flat regimes, where nothing helps. Nor are the calibrated quantities idle: the per-cell $z$ predicts the pooled advantage at least as well as raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ (NC $-0.50$ vs $-0.45$; FS $-0.36$ vs $-0.18$), and within transfer datasets the excess predicts at $-0.55$..$-0.84$, inverting only on ImageNet. They are not an operational policy: gating on $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ underperforms the label-flatness gate and always-cosine on NC ($+0.19$/$+0.29$ vs $+0.34$/$+0.51$ and $+0.48$/$+0.59$pp) and is the weakest policy on FS ($+0.08$/$+0.13$pp; Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). On NC, at the headline $t$ the projection does not improve on average ($-0.24$pp over 60 cells, $-0.00$ hierarchical, with significant losses in several cells): the gate's value is \emph{avoiding} the projection where labels are flat (always-H loses $-0.70$pp there; the gated rule nothing), while cosine is a safe default ($+0.48$pp mean). Robustness to the projection target: the FS advantage is positive at every $t$ tested (the a-priori $t=1/\sqrt2$ is conservative); NC is $t$-sensitive, its mean crossing zero near the headline target, consistent with NC gains concentrating in the DINOv2 cells. % exp2 + exp20 + exp24 policy tables; night/t_sweep.csv

\paragraph{Transfer and negatives.} Transplanted to DBpedia (three-level text hierarchy, 219 classes, three embedders), the rule predicts correctly on both sides: genuine excess and $0.88$ sibling-triplet alignment, yet $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ above the low band and indeed no meaningful gain (FS $+0.04$..$+0.08$pp at a $\sim$99\% ceiling; NC $-0.4$..$-0.8$pp). % exp14_dbpedia.csv
Sample-level tasks do not benefit anywhere: kNN loses $2.6$pp on average and sample retrieval improves in 0/60 cells, because the detected hierarchy lives at the prototype level while the radial map distorts local neighborhoods, consistent with the weak sample-level excesses of Appendix~\ref{app:sample}. The domain of validity is prototype-based metric tasks; typical gains are $+0.5$ to $+2$pp at zero cost. % exp15, tasks_t707

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the census establishes.} Tree-like form beyond matched nulls is widespread but not universal: near-universal across the vision backbones tested (ViTs and two ConvNets), recipe- and scale-dependent in text, always relative to a concept set. Its content is substantially shared: all recipes recover the human taxonomy under every non-degenerate configuration, supervision deepens the resemblance, and the naive islands are a measurement artifact. Form and content even anti-align across families: the models with the most WordNet content (supervised ViTs, CLIP) carry the most fragile form (supremum-borne), while the family with the most robust form (DINOv2) is the least WordNet-aligned; raw readings of $\delta$, of $\xi$, or of tree similarity are not interpretable without the instrument. Excess measures existence while raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts exploitability; they dissociate in both directions (DINOv2/ImageNet: supremum excess at null yet gaining, a real but non-WordNet tree, \S\ref{sec:form}; DBpedia embedders: genuine excess, no gain), the corollary uses the raw descriptor, the excess is the existence proof, and the dissociation is localized to ImageNet.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~The spectrum excess certifies structure beyond second moments, star-like clustering included; $\delta$ cannot separate a star from a deeper tree (Appendix~\ref{app:star}), so depth rests on the class-subsampling control and content on \S\ref{sec:content}. (ii)~The census is class-centroid geometry on six datasets; other concept sets may expose different structure. (iii)~Causal-LM values (not the scale ordering) are template-conditioned. (iv)~The objective--geometry link is correlational (triplet-bounded, not closed); the within-dataset $\delta$--gain prediction rests chiefly on DINOv2's scale range (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). (v)~Downstream scope is prototype-based metric tasks with modest gains. (vi)~The parallelogram descriptor $\xi$ tracks norm dispersion rather than curvature (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}) and is reported only as such.

\subsubsection*{Ethics Statement}
This work analyzes publicly released models and standard public datasets, involves no human subjects and no personal data, and proposes an analysis methodology and a metric-selection heuristic. We do not foresee ethical risks beyond those already associated with the underlying models and datasets.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every number in the paper is annotated in the source with the result file that produces it; all tables are generated by script from those files, and the full pipeline (feature extraction, diagnostics, nulls, calibration, tasks, statistics, figures) with fixed seeds will be released. Estimator settings are stated in \S\ref{sec:instrument}. Computing $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ on 1000 centroids takes seconds on CPU; the full task grid runs in under 30 minutes on one consumer GPU.

\subsubsection*{The Use of Large Language Models}
LLM-based assistants were used, under continuous author direction and review, to draft and edit text, to write analysis scripts, and to run the experiment pipeline on the authors' hardware. All experimental designs, results, verifications, and final claims were checked and approved by the authors, who take full responsibility for the content of this paper.

\subsection{Parallelogram defect $\xi$: a norm-structure descriptor}
\label{app:xi}
For a triangle $(a,b,c)$ with $m$ the approximate midpoint of $(b,c)$ (the minimizer of $D(\cdot,b){+}D(\cdot,c){+}|D(\cdot,b){-}D(\cdot,c)|$), the parallelogram defect is $\xi = \big(D(a,m)^2 + \tfrac14 D(b,c)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,b)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,c)^2\big)\,/\,(2\,D(a,m)\,\mathrm{diam})$, averaged over $2{\times}10^4$ sampled triangles: $0$ in Euclidean space, positive on spheres, negative in hyperbolic space and on trees.
