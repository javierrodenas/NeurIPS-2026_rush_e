\begin{abstract}
Foundation models are often said to converge toward shared representations. We ask this question of geometry: is a model's arrangement of classes tree-like, and is that tree shared? Raw tree-likeness (Gromov $\delta$) is confounded by dimension, class count and covariance, so we build a calibrated instrument that reads every score as an excess over a random cloud with the same dimension and spectrum, and we validate its readings against alternative null constructions and statistics. Across 12 vision backbones, 6 datasets and 16 text models the answer is nuanced. Beyond-null, clustered structure is present in most cells and every family: genuine in 49 of 72 vision cells and 18 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100, with a negative excess in 70 of 72; in text it is robust at the larger GPT-2 scales and fragile at the smaller, present throughout Pythia and OLMo, and absent for sentence embedders on class names yet present for the same embedders on a real text hierarchy. A matched-star depth test, validated for class sets of ImageNet's size, finds hierarchy above the WordNet superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones; on smaller class sets it is not validated. The trees are moderately shared: a naive comparison of the models' hierarchies isolates the self-supervised family as an island, a property of the clustering cut and of the Euclidean configuration; under cut-free measures with angular configurations the family is indistinguishable from the rest, and every training recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision. What foundation models share is clustered structure and a partial, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy --- not one common tree.
\end{abstract}

Semantic knowledge is hierarchical: instances belong to categories (a husky is a dog), categories to superordinate classes (dogs are mammals), and so on upward. The natural geometric home for such hierarchies is hyperbolic space \citep{nickel2017poincare, sarkar2011low}, and a long line of work \emph{imposes} it at training time \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, chen2022fully, desai2023meru}. A complementary observation, latent hyperbolicity, holds that standard Euclidean models already organize concepts in a tree-like metric structure \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, yang2025hyperbolic}. Is that structure hierarchical, and for whom?

The na\"ive measurement of this question is confounded. Gromov $\delta$-hyperbolicity \citep{Gromov1987} assigns $0$ to trees, but three artifacts push raw readings down without any hierarchy: high-dimensional clouds concentrate toward equidistance (a degenerate star tree), anisotropic spectra mimic low-dimensional behavior, and small class counts shrink the quadruple population. These artifacts are real: small GPT-2 models sit \emph{at} their matched null despite low raw $\delta$, and classic sentence embedders share raw $\delta$ with vision-contrastive models while carrying none of their genuine structure. % exp18_text_nulls.csv

Our first contribution is an \textbf{instrument}: the exact estimator calibrated on reference geometries (trees, hyperbolic regions, spheres, Gaussians of growing dimension), with every reading expressed as an \emph{excess} over a null cloud matched in dimension and covariance spectrum and ranked against 200 null replicates. With it we ask of geometry what convergence work asks of similarity \citep{huh2024platonic, groger2026aristotelian}: what survives calibration, and is it shared --- is there a Platonic tree? Comparing the resulting trees across models turns out to need a calibration of its own (\S\ref{sec:content}).

\textbf{What survives is beyond-null, clustered structure.} It is genuine in 49 of 72 vision cells and 18 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100, with a negative excess in 70 of 72: most cells and every family. In text it is robust at the larger GPT-2 scales and fragile at the smaller, and a backbone trained in hyperbolic space shows the same clustered arrangement as its Euclidean twin. A matched-star test, validated at ImageNet's class count, finds hierarchy above the WordNet superclasses in 4 of 12 backbones (ViT-S/B/L and DINOv2-L) and is not validated on the smaller class sets. % expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR53_text_haar_p999_200.csv, expR51_meru_control.csv, expR56_depth_variants.csv

\textbf{Sharing is graded, and its naive measurement misleads.} A naive tree-to-tree comparison isolates the label-free family as a categorical island, a property of the clustering cut and of the Euclidean configuration: once the configuration is controlled, ImageNet agreement at the cut rises from $0.03$ to $0.38$, and under cut-free triplet agreement with angular configurations the family is indistinguishable from the rest. Every training recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision, and no configuration returns one common tree. % exp22, exp23_treemap_controls.npz

\textbf{Our contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A calibrated instrument for class geometry.} Estimator calibration on reference geometries, spectrum-matched nulls read as BH-corrected percentile ranks, validated against alternative null constructions and statistics, and a class-count control. Identical raw readings receive opposite verdicts, so the raw scale is not interpretable.
\item \textbf{A calibrated census.} Genuine beyond-null structure in most vision cells and every family and, in text, structure that depends on recipe and is robust only at scale. A star calibration shows that the excess certifies clustering, not depth; a matched-star test, validated at $n{=}1000$, certifies depth above the superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones.
\item \textbf{The map of trees.} The naive comparison manufactures a categorical island, a property of the cut. Cut-free measures and the controlled comparison show graded, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy.
\item \textbf{A scoped corollary} (appendix): the same calibrated descriptors indicate where non-Euclidean readouts pay on prototype-based tasks.
\end{enumerate}
What is new, in order: the matched-star depth test with its validation, a backbone trained in hyperbolic space as a control, and the recipe- and scale-dependence of text; the raw-$\delta$ confound itself is not (\S\ref{sec:related}).


\section{Related Work and Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperbolic deep learning: imposing vs measuring.} Building on tree embeddability \citep{sarkar2011low} and Poincar\'{e} embeddings \citep{nickel2017poincare, ganea2018hyperbolic}, hyperbolic methods span vision \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, ermolov2022hyperbolic, atigh2022hyperbolic, bdeir2024fully}, vision--language \citep{desai2023meru, pal2025compositional}, graphs \citep{chami2019hyperbolic}, and language \citep{chen2022fully, he2026helm, sinha2024learning}. Latent hyperbolicity, low measured $\delta$ in standard models, has been reported for CNN/ViT features \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully}, token embeddings \citep{yang2025hyperbolic}, and word vectors \citep{tifrea2019poincare}. Those readings are sample-level and uncalibrated; transplanted to sample-level features, our instrument finds excesses mostly within null noise (Appendix~\ref{app:sample}), and our census is about inter-class (centroid) geometry.

\paragraph{$\delta$ against a random null, in network science.} Measuring Gromov $\delta$ on large networks and comparing it with random graphs of matched size and degree sequence is an established practice \citep{narayan2011curvature, kennedy2013hyperbolicity, adcock2013treelike}, and those studies already found that raw $\delta$ alone does not certify tree-likeness. The raw-$\delta$ confound is therefore not our contribution. What is new here is the instrument for embedding clouds rather than graphs: spectrum-matched nulls at the resolution of the null replicate, a depth test against matched stars, and a calibration of the tree-to-tree comparison itself.

\paragraph{Geometric analyses of representations.} Representation similarity \citep{kornblith2019cka}, intrinsic dimension \citep{ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic}, and neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} characterize Euclidean structure without testing tree-likeness; \S\ref{sec:form} relates our excess to the collapsed simplex. Estimating Gromov hyperbolicity on sampled quadruples has known bias and scale caveats \citep{fournier2015computing}, which our calibration-plus-null design addresses operationally.

\paragraph{Representational convergence.} The Platonic Representation Hypothesis \citep{huh2024platonic} argues that models converge toward shared representations; \citet{koepke2026cave} stress-test cross-modal convergence at scale, and \citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that permutation-calibrated similarity removes most apparent global convergence while local neighborhood structure survives. \citet{park2024geometry} read hierarchical concept structure in LMs from the orthogonality of concept directions; our text census is a complementary, metric-level reading of the same question. We are the geometric complement of this program: the same calibration philosophy applied within models and, in \S\ref{sec:content}, across them.

\paragraph{Background.} For four points $w,x,y,z$ of a metric space, sort the three pairing sums $d(w,x){+}d(y,z)$, $d(w,y){+}d(x,z)$, $d(w,z){+}d(x,y)$ as $S_{\max}\ge S_{\mathrm{mid}}\ge S_{\min}$; the four-point defect is $(S_{\max}-S_{\mathrm{mid}})/2$ and $\delta$ is its supremum. Trees have $\delta=0$; under this four-point definition the hyperbolic plane has $\delta=\ln 2\approx0.693$ \citep{nica2016strong}, distinct from the thin-triangles constant $\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.881$. We use the normalized $\delta_{\text{norm}}=\delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ throughout: on this scale hyperbolic regions drift to $0$ as they grow while spheres stay high, so the intuitive ordering trees $<$ hyperbolic $<$ spherical holds. Ollivier--Ricci curvature \citep{ollivier2009ricci} on a kNN graph is our local companion: positive on tightly clustered neighborhoods, negative on tree-like bridges.

\label{sec:instrument}

This section builds the measurement layer in whose units every later claim is stated: raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is interpretable only against calibrated references and matched nulls.

\paragraph{Classes are centroids; a cell is a (model, class set) pair.} Each frozen model is a fixed feature extractor; classes are represented by centroids (vision: the mean of each class's cached training embeddings, $100$ on ImageNet, the full cached split of $80$--$6{,}000$ elsewhere; text: embeddings of ``a photo of a \{class\}'' over the 1000 ImageNet classes, plus the corpus variations of \S\ref{sec:form}). The calibrated census covers 12 vision backbones $\times$ 6 datasets (a \emph{cell} is one model$\times$dataset pair) and 16 text models; the task grid covers the 10 backbones with cached per-dataset features (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}).
Estimator settings: the 99.9th-percentile four-point statistic $\hat\delta_{99.9}$ is the reading of record and the supremum, the pre-registered reading, is reported in the appendix (the reason is stated below); $5{\times}10^5$ quadruples per seed, 10 seeds (exact at $C{=}10$, where the draws cover every quadruple many times over); ORC on $k{=}10$ kNN graphs. On the transfer sets the excess is budget-stable from $10^5$ quadruples and image-resampling moves it far less than its size; on ImageNet every sign holds at every budget but magnitudes drift by up to $0.02$ (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{A low raw value is not evidence.} On reference geometries (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}) the estimator recovers the tree zero and the $\ln 2$ four-point constant of $\mathbb{H}^2$ ($\delta_{\text{norm}}\to0$ as hyperbolic regions grow), spheres stay high ($0.14$--$0.18$), and iid Gaussians drift low as $d$ grows ($0.104\to0.046$ from $d{=}192$ to $1536$): the dimension confound.

\paragraph{The reading is an excess over a matched null.} For every (model, class set) cell we synthesize null clouds with the same $n$, $d$, mean and covariance \emph{spectrum} (the Haar construction: random orthogonal coefficients recombined with the real singular values, so the sample spectrum is exact; the Gaussian-coefficient construction is the alternative reported in Appendix~\ref{app:robust}), and report the \textbf{excess} $\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{real}}-\delta_{\text{norm}}^{\text{null}}$ over 200 spectrum-null replicates (20 for $\xi$). The primary evidence is the \emph{percentile rank} $r$/200, the number of replicates above the real value, and its left-tail $p=(1+\#\{\text{null}\le\text{real}\})/201$; a cell is \emph{genuine} when its Benjamini--Hochberg-corrected $p$ across the census is $\le0.05$. Two choices changed after pre-registration and are disclosed here. The supremum statistic did not converge under the quadruple budget at $C{=}1000$ and disagreed with the 99.9th percentile of the same defects in both directions with $p<0.01$ (Table~\ref{tab:b21-2x2}); the distributional statistic is therefore the reading of record and the supremum is reported in full in the appendix. The Gaussian-coefficient null does not reproduce the sample spectrum it claims to match when $n$ is small relative to $d$ and inflates excesses for low-rank clouds, whereas the Haar construction (random orthogonal coefficients, exact sample spectrum) does reproduce it (Table~\ref{tab:b27-nullvariants}); the Haar null is the null of record and both are reported. Rank-based calibration is what gives permutation scores their finite-sample validity \citep[\S5.1]{groger2026aristotelian}; a $z$-score on a handful of replicates is not rank-based, so $z$ is kept only as a secondary summary in the appendix. The null is aggregation-aware in the sense of \citet[\S5.2]{groger2026aristotelian}: the same statistic, the 99.9th percentile of the same $5{\times}10^5$ sampled defects per seed, is evaluated on every null replicate, so the max-statistic inflation is calibrated rather than ignored; the real cloud averages 10 quadruple seeds and each replicate 5 (3 in the text census), which affects variance, not bias. The instrument is a single script: given an $n{\times}d$ centroid matrix and, optionally, superclass labels, it returns the raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$, the excess with its rank and $p$ over 200 replicates, and the matched-star depth test. A negative excess is called \emph{beyond-null (clustered) structure}; ``tree-like'' is reserved for the depth test of \S\ref{sec:form}; ``genuine'' means BH-corrected $p\le0.05$ against the Haar spectrum-matched null, one hypothesis of ``no hierarchy'' (each null's scope: Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}). Sign-positive excess means \emph{less} tree-like than chance given the spectrum.

\paragraph{$\xi$ reads norm structure, not curvature.} A parallelogram estimator $\xi$, intended as a curvature sign, is driven negative by heterogeneous norms alone, so it is reported only as a norm-structure descriptor (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}). % exp12, expR43_xi_norm_references.csv

\paragraph{Class count is a measured variable, not a nuisance.} Because $C$ sets the quadruple population, we sweep ImageNet subsets $C\in\{10,\dots,1000\}$ with two compositions: random (spanning the full hierarchy) and WordNet-coherent (siblings, effectively \emph{flat}). Results in \S\ref{sec:form}.

\paragraph{Depth above the labelled clusters: certified on ImageNet, not on CIFAR-100.} A matched star tests hierarchy \emph{above} the labelled superclass clusters: Gaussian hubs with the real hub radius and, within each cluster, a Haar sample with the cluster's own covariance (10 star seeds), compared with the real centroids under the hub-randomizing null, which keeps every frame cluster intact, so the test certifies hierarchy above the frame only (Table~\ref{tab:b34-depthvariants}). With the frame at the finest clusters, our pre-set bar (power $\ge0.8$ for two- and three-level hierarchies at noise ratios $\le0.3$ for both $n$, false alarms $\le5$\% each way) fails at $n{=}100$, where pure stars with $K\ge12$ leaf clusters (under ten points per cluster) are declared more hierarchical in 17\% of runs, and is met at $n{=}1000$: power 1.00 at ratios $\le0.3$ and 0.90 at $0.6$, no false alarm either way (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}b; Table~\ref{tab:b35-power}). We certify the test within that regime only, a scoping adopted after the sweep. On ImageNet ($n{=}1000$, WordNet $K{=}30$) the real cloud is more hierarchical than its matched star beyond the noise in 4 of 12 backbones, ViT-S/B/L and DINOv2-L ($z$ from $-2.4$ to $-4.0$), not in the other eight, and never less (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}a); CIFAR-100 ($n{=}100$, $K{=}20$) lies in the unvalidated regime and its readings stay unvalidated (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). An earlier isotropic star, blind to each cluster's covariance, gave $z$ up to $+6.8$ on CIFAR-100 and 16\% false alarms on synthetic stars and is withdrawn; the hierarchically fine-tuned checkpoints of Appendix~\ref{app:interventions} were not stored, so that positive control could not be run. % expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR55b_depth_power_leafframe.csv, expR55_depth_power.csv

\paragraph{The angular reading agrees.} Because \S\ref{sec:content} finds DINOv2's semantics in the angular component, we repeat the census on cosine geometry (L2-normalized centroids, geodesic distances, the same Haar null on the normalized cloud): 56 of 72 cells genuine, 22 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100, and the verdict agrees with the Euclidean record in 65 of 72 cells; DINOv2 on ImageNet is genuine under both. It is reported as robustness, never as the record (Table~\ref{tab:b32-cosine}). % expR57_census_cosine_haar_p999_200.csv

\paragraph{The excess certifies clustering, not depth.} The spectrum excess measures structure beyond second moments, and a pure star already has such structure: a synthetic 30-cluster star with no hierarchy scores $\approx-0.11$, as deep as any real cell (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). A backbone \emph{trained} in hyperbolic space is no exception: MERU \citep{desai2023meru} and its Euclidean CLIP twin show the same clustering excess and MERU's Lorentz-metric $\hat\delta$ matches its Euclidean value; its depth reading used the withdrawn isotropic star and is not part of the certified result (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). The excess measures how classes are arranged, not the geometry they are embedded in; \emph{whose} tree it is is the subject of \S\ref{sec:content}. % expR42_star_calibration.csv, expR51_meru_control.csv

\paragraph{Relation to neural collapse.} Neural collapse \citep{papyan2020prevalence} predicts that the class means of a supervised classifier converge to a simplex equiangular tight frame: equal norms, equal pairwise angles. An exact ETF is the extreme case of the star caveat: every four-point defect zero, $\delta=0$ with no hierarchy. Our reading differs in two ways. The arrangement we certify is not flat: class means cluster around superclass hubs, which an ETF would erase; cutting the centroid dendrograms recovers the CIFAR-100 superclasses (\S\ref{sec:content}) and negative curvature concentrates on the bridges between clusters, which is why a high mean ORC coexists with beyond-null structure (Appendix~\ref{app:orc}). And the same clustered arrangement appears beyond the null in models trained without class labels (DINO/DINOv2, CLIP/SigLIP), on transfer datasets, and in causal LMs, outside the setting where neural collapse is stated; the depth test asks what neural collapse does not, whether there is hierarchy above the hubs, and answers yes for four ImageNet backbones, three of them supervised ViTs. % night/orc_bridges.csv

\paragraph{Text: recipe and scale decide, and the probe matters.} On DBpedia, a real three-level text hierarchy, the sentence embedders show beyond-null excess under the record ($-0.017$ to $-0.021$, every replicate above the real value; Table~\ref{tab:a9}); on the harder probe, the 1000 ImageNet class names, the same embedders are not genuine under the record (excess $+0.002$ to $+0.004$) despite raw values indistinguishable from vision backbones whose excess is (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}b): presence of structure is a property of the (model, concept set) pair. Within causal LMs on the class-name probe (Figure~\ref{fig:textnulls}), GPT-2 L and XL are genuine under all four null$\times$statistic constructions and under cosine, whereas S and M flip across readings (S genuine under the record, $p=0.02$; M not, $p=0.15$): robust at the larger scales, fragile at the smaller. Pythia is genuine at every scale tested, and so is OLMo-1B. Extraction is part of the measurement: left-padded batching moves GPT-2's $\hat\delta$ by up to $0.02$, of the order of the fragile excesses; the padding-free protocol is the record (Table~\ref{tab:b28-extraction}). Genuineness is not a proxy for anisotropy: GTE-base has the highest effective rank and is not genuine. The only LLM-backboned embedder tested (GTE-Qwen2-1.5B) is not genuine under the record (Table~\ref{tab:b23-text200}). Causal-LM values are template-sensitive but the scale ordering is template-robust (Appendix~\ref{app:templates}). % expR53_text_haar_p999_200.csv, expR53_text_haar_sup_200.csv, expR53_text_gauss_p999_200.csv, expR48b_text_census200_bs1.csv, expR57_text_cosine_haar_p999_200.csv, expR61_dbpedia_record.csv, exp18b_text_anisotropy.csv, expR49_template_nulls_bs1.csv

\paragraph{Hierarchy depth, not class count.} At fixed $C$, random ImageNet subsets, which span the full hierarchy, are more exploitable than WordNet-coherent sibling subsets, an effectively flat concept set, and carry more beyond-null structure at small $C$ (CLIP-L at every $C$, DINOv2-L up to $C{=}100$, DINOv2-G up to $C{=}20$; beyond that the two compositions converge; Table~\ref{tab:b3-csweep}). Random subsets still score $-0.08$ to $-0.14$ at $C{=}10$, so MNIST's flat verdict is not a class-count artifact. DINOv2-G stays below its null at every $C$ (excess $-0.008$ at $C{=}1000$, $r{=}200$), where the supremum had placed it at the null. % expR60_c_sweep_record.csv, exp19_c_sweep.csv

\paragraph{The form tracks training.} Two interventions move it (Figure~\ref{fig:causal}, Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}; raw $\delta$, within architecture): fine-tuning on a task with no class hierarchy raises $\delta$ by 19--91\%, and $\delta$ falls across transformer depth. Shuffling the embedding-to-label correspondence leaves it unchanged, as a null-invariant quantity should. Random-weight networks give higher raw $\delta$ but near-Gaussian features, so they are not counted as evidence; excess-based versions of these interventions await stored point clouds. On matched architecture (ViT-B supervised/DINO/CLIP) all three objectives yield genuine form on most datasets, and the family separations emerge with scale rather than at fixed size. % analysis4_finetuning.csv, e1_delta_by_layer.csv, e5_random_control.csv, night/arch_matched_triplet.csv


\section{Findings II: Shared Content, Fragile Measurement}
\label{sec:content}

If the measured trees were a shared representation of reality, models should agree with each other and with human taxonomy; whether they appear to depends on how the trees are measured.

\paragraph{The naive map manufactures an island.} We extract each model's hierarchy explicitly (a dendrogram over the shared centroids) and compare hierarchies, not representations, by ARI between cut partitions (adjusted Rand index, chance-corrected partition agreement) and by the cophenetic correlation (CPCC) between tree and distances. Under the naive configuration, Euclidean distances with average linkage, the map (Figure~\ref{fig:treemap}) is spectacular: supervised ViTs and contrastive VLMs form one block (cross-family ARI up to $0.82$), while the label-free DINOv2 family appears as an island (ARI $\le 0.06$ against every supervised model). The island is a property of the cut and of the Euclidean configurations: without a cut it persists in the naive configuration (cophenetic correlation $0.36$ for DINOv2-B/L/G vs the block against $0.80$ within the block; triplet agreement $0.47$ vs $0.74$), but under angular configurations DINOv2's triplet agreement with the block equals the block's own ($0.77$ vs $0.76$; Table~\ref{tab:b33-cutfree}). % exp22_tree_similarity_{imagenet,cifar100}.npz, expR58_treemap_cutfree_summary.csv
The categorical island, however, is a measurement artifact. Its cuts are degenerate: DINOv2's Euclidean average-linkage dendrograms chain (up to $97\%$ of classes in one cluster), forcing ARI $\approx 0$ by construction, and Euclidean distance is the metric our diagnostics flag as mismatched for this family (\S\ref{sec:form}). % exp23_treemap_controls.npz
A configuration must be selected by a criterion fixed \emph{without reference to the agreements being compared}. Cophenetic fidelity alone re-selects the chaining configuration, so we fix: \emph{highest CPCC among configurations whose reference cuts are non-degenerate for every model} (largest cluster $\le 50\%$); it selects cosine-average on ImageNet and cosine-complete on CIFAR-100 (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). The $0.5$ degeneracy bound is the criterion's one free parameter: across admissible bounds the ImageNet gap ranges from $0.13$ to $0.38$, never back to the categorical island ($\le0.06$; Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). % threshold sensitivity, exp23_config_diagnostics.json
Under the selected configurations (Figures~\ref{fig:treemap}b and \ref{fig:treemapc100}b) the island shrinks from categorical to moderate at the cut (ImageNet $0.38$ vs block $0.48$) and closes under triplet agreement ($0.77$ vs $0.76$) while a gap remains in cophenetic correlation ($0.48$ vs $0.78$). Across \emph{all} non-degenerate configurations DINOv2 recovers the true 20 CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI $0.2$--$0.5$, reaching the supervised band only under cosine-Ward, the configuration where the CIFAR-100 island also closes fully (per-configuration values in Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). % exp23, per-config ground-truth checks

\paragraph{The island is an artifact; sharing is graded.} Three statements hold across every non-degenerate configuration. The categorical island is an artifact. Label-free models recover the human taxonomy partially, and supervision deepens the resemblance: paired within each admissible configuration, the label-supervised ViT recovers more than each DINOv2 model in 11 of 12 comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). Tree-to-tree comparison itself is fragile to configuration. A moderate label-free gap persists on ImageNet at the cut under every configuration and in cophenetic correlation, but not in triplet agreement under angular configurations: the gap is a property of the measure as much as of the models.

\paragraph{WordNet alignment follows supervision, not the excess.} Spearman correlation between inter-centroid distances and WordNet tree distances on ImageNet: $+0.57$ to $+0.59$ for CLIP/SigLIP, $+0.49$ to $+0.53$ for supervised ViTs, $+0.36$ for DINO-B, and only $+0.18$ to $+0.22$ for DINOv2, genuine on ImageNet and both CIFAR sets under the record, with shuffle and Gaussian controls at $\approx0$ (Table~\ref{tab:census}); across the three families, WordNet content and robustness of the excess anti-correlate, supervised and contrastive the most aligned and the most fragile, DINOv2 the least aligned and the most robust. % exp3_alignment.csv
Cutting centroid dendrograms recovers the 20 true CIFAR-100 superclasses at ARI up to $0.61$ (permuted labels $\approx0$). % exp8_p1_recovery.csv
On HierarCaps \citep{alper2024hierarcaps} (1000 four-level caption chains), text encoders show the same reading with no class set: the projection radius orders levels within a chain, sibling triplets are resolved at $0.83$--$0.97$ (chance $0.5$), and the leaf excess repeats the text census (Table~\ref{tab:a8}). % exp16_hierarcaps.csv
Three controls bound circularity: WordNet-coherent groupings have lower raw $\delta$ than random ones in 11/12 models; single-image distances still correlate with WordNet ($\rho$ up to $+0.26$); and the decoupling above is the opposite of label injection. % exp1, exp8_p6_pooling.csv
DINOv2's semantics concentrate in the angular component (sibling-triplet agreement $0.91$ under cosine vs $0.71$ Euclidean for DINOv2-L; Appendix~\ref{app:tables}), which is why Euclidean dendrograms chain for this family into the naive island. This is consistent with the census: DINOv2's ImageNet structure is genuine under the record and the least aligned with WordNet's top level, the form-vs-content split. % exp10_local_vs_global.csv

\paragraph{The recovery is not WordNet circularity.} ImageNet classes are WordNet synsets, so recovering WordNet there could be circular. Repeating the tree map on DBpedia Classes, a three-level ontology independent of WordNet (219 leaf classes; the three text embedders), closes this door: no configuration degenerates, the embedders recover the true 70-way level-2 partition at ARI $0.21$--$0.29$ in all six configurations and agree with each other at cross-model ARI $0.65$--$0.82$. The recovered hierarchy is real, shared and configuration-robust; the fragility above is a property of the (family, concept set) pair. % exp27_dbpedia_treemap.json

\paragraph{Local agreement survives calibration.} We apply the permutation calibration of \citet{groger2026aristotelian}: for each of the 66 model pairs, $K{=}200$ permutations of the class correspondence give the null distribution, the threshold $\tau_{0.05}$ is its $\lceil 0.95(K{+}1)\rceil$-th order statistic, and the calibrated score is $\max\{(s-\tau_{0.05})/(1-\tau_{0.05}),0\}$. Local neighborhood agreement survives: mutual-kNN is $0.472$ raw and $0.464$ calibrated, with every pair at $p=1/201$, and the null mean $0.010$ equals the chance level $10/999$. Centroid CKA survives too ($0.633$ raw, $0.577$ calibrated, null mean $0.108$). The Poincar\'{e} reading improves neither agreement (kNN $0.472\to0.425$; CKA $0.633\to0.640$): hyperbolic distances buy no alignment (Table~\ref{tab:b31-groger}). % exp21b_local_global_K200.csv

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the census establishes.} Foundation models organize classes into beyond-null, clustered structure and partially recover the human taxonomy, more so with supervision; they do not converge to one common tree. The structure is present in most cells and every family (49 of 72; 18 of 24 on ImageNet and CIFAR-100), recipe- and scale-dependent in text, always relative to a concept set; depth above the WordNet superclasses is certified in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones. Its content is moderately shared and configuration-dependent: all recipes recover the human taxonomy partially under every non-degenerate configuration, the residual gap on ImageNet ranges from $0.13$ to $0.38$ with the comparison threshold, and the naive islands are a measurement artifact. Raw readings of $\delta$, $\xi$ or tree similarity are not interpretable without the instrument; excess measures existence while raw $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts exploitability, and the two dissociate.

\paragraph{A corollary, in the appendix.} The same calibrated readings diagnose where a zero-cost non-Euclidean readout pays on prototype-based tasks: the Poincar\'{e} advantage tracks $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ within each dataset, the objective picks the metric, and cosine is a safe default (Appendix~\ref{app:corollary}). % exp2, exp24, table1_regenerated

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~The spectrum excess certifies structure beyond second moments, star-like clustering included; the matched-star test certifies hierarchy above its frame only and is validated for $n\approx1000$ (not below about ten points per cluster), so depth is certified on ImageNet alone; hierarchy within the labelled clusters is not tested (Appendix~\ref{app:star}), and the hierarchy's content rests on \S\ref{sec:content}. (ii)~The census is class-centroid geometry on six datasets; other concept sets may expose different structure. (iii)~Causal-LM values (not the scale ordering) are template-conditioned. (iv)~The objective--geometry link is correlational (triplet-bounded, not closed); the within-dataset $\delta$--gain prediction rests chiefly on DINOv2's scale range (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). (v)~Downstream scope is prototype-based metric tasks with modest gains. (vi)~The parallelogram descriptor $\xi$ tracks norm dispersion rather than curvature (Appendix~\ref{app:xi}) and is reported only as such. (vii)~A causal LM's class representation depends on the extraction protocol as much as on the model: left-padded batching moves $\hat\delta$ by up to $0.018$, of the order of the L/XL excess.

\subsection{The corollary: diagnosing exploitability}
\label{app:corollary}
\paragraph{One parameter-free projection and two tasks serve the corollary.} The parameter-free map $\phi(x)=\tanh(\tfrac12\|s(x-\mu)\|)\cdot\frac{s(x-\mu)}{\|s(x-\mu)\|}$ centers features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$, and preserves directions; prototypes in the ball are means of projected points re-mapped through the origin. Tasks: nearest-centroid (NC) and 5-way 5-shot (FS, 1000 paired episodes); metric arms: Euclidean (R), Poincar\'{e} (H), the radial map with Euclidean distances (RT), cosine (COS), and the L2-normalized stack. Changing the readout metric on the intact configuration, which is what the rule selects among, is distinct from deforming the configuration itself. % exp1, exp2

\paragraph{$\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts the gain within datasets.} Within each dataset the Poincar\'{e} advantage tracks $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ (NC $r=-0.83$ on ImageNet) and survives partialing out dimension, baseline accuracy, family and spectral covariates; without DINOv2 it holds on ImageNet and CIFAR-100 but not on CIFAR-10/DTD (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). Pooled cross-dataset correlations are family-driven and not used as evidence; on flat-label datasets no metric helps. Gains carry paired confidence intervals and a McNemar test (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). % table1_regenerated, exp20, night/correlation_cis, exp13

\paragraph{The training objective picks the metric.} The radial map with Euclidean distances changes nothing, so the gain is the metric itself, and the split follows the objective: cosine suffices for DINOv2, Poincar\'{e} $\approx$ cosine for supervised ViTs, and Poincar\'{e} beats cosine for CLIP by up to $+1.3$pp even after L2 normalization (Figure~\ref{fig:bestmetric} in Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}; held-out selection and policy comparison in this appendix). The rule is read off the families it is scored on: it describes them rather than validating a policy. % exp2, exp2b, exp24

\paragraph{A diagnostic, not a policy.} Cosine is a safe default, gating on $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is the weakest policy tested (this appendix), and gains are modest and limited to prototype-based tasks (sample-level tasks never benefit, Appendix~\ref{app:sample}). On DBpedia, a three-level text hierarchy, the diagnostic predicts correctly on both sides: genuine excess, no metric gain. % exp2, exp20, exp24, exp14, exp15

\subsection{Parallelogram defect $\xi$: a norm-structure descriptor}
\label{app:xi}
For a triangle $(a,b,c)$ with $m$ the approximate midpoint of $(b,c)$ (the minimizer of $D(\cdot,b){+}D(\cdot,c){+}|D(\cdot,b){-}D(\cdot,c)|$), the parallelogram defect is $\xi = \big(D(a,m)^2 + \tfrac14 D(b,c)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,b)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,c)^2\big)\,/\,(2\,D(a,m)\,\mathrm{diam})$, averaged over $2{\times}10^4$ sampled triangles: $0$ in Euclidean space, positive on spheres, negative in hyperbolic space and on trees.
