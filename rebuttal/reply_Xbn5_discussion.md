Thank you for the candid assessment. Before the final decision, we would like to offer one clarification on each of the three narrowings, because we believe two of them are narrowings of language rather than of contribution.

**"Cosine distance is often competitive."** Cosine is itself a non-Euclidean geometry (the spherical geodesic on L2-normalized features; unlike the sphericizing control in our rebuttal, which deforms the configuration and raises $\hat\delta$, it changes only the readout metric), so we tested whether the diagnostic predicts the advantage of the best zero-cost metric over Euclidean rather than only H−R: the correlation survives (FS r = −0.31 pooled, −0.33 within hierarchical datasets; the same $\hat\delta$ also predicts COS−R at r = −0.39). The most tree-like family has the largest exploitable gain, growing S→G from +0.6..+0.8pp to +2.4..+2.6pp (FS, hierarchical datasets, per-cell values; the −0.5pp H−COS reported to Reviewer RJje is a family mean, and the cosine gap widens with scale) exactly as $\hat\delta$ falls, and on MNIST the correlation vanishes for every metric (cosine +0.2pp on average; residual offsets uncorrelated with $\hat\delta$, as in Fig. 11). The tool becomes two-step, both steps free: $\hat\delta$/ORC decide whether to leave Euclidean; the training objective selects which metric (SSL → cosine; contrastive VLM → Poincaré, +0.9..+1.3pp over cosine on CIFAR-100/10/DTD even after L2 normalization, ImageNet +0.1..+0.2pp). We believe this, together with the DBpedia transfer, is the "continue to predict ... beyond NC and FS vision classification" test the review asked significance to rest on.

**"Gains limited primarily to prototype-based tasks."** This boundary is not a product of the discussion: the submission's Limitations section already restricted the downstream claims to the two prototype-based tasks.
