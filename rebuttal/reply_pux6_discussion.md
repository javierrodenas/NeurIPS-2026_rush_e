On Q3, we believe the apparent inversion dissolves once cosine is recognized as a geometry rather than a neutral baseline: cosine ranking is the spherical geodesic on L2-normalized features, so "cosine suffices for DINOv2" means DINOv2's tree is harvested by an angular non-Euclidean metric, not that the structure is unexploitable. (No tension with the sphericizing control in our rebuttal to Reviewer RJje: that operation deforms the centroid configuration itself and raises $\hat\delta$, whereas here the configuration is kept and only the readout metric changes.) We verified this on the same 60-cell grid:

1. **$\hat\delta$ predicts the exploitable non-Euclidean gain, not only H−R.** Replacing H−R with the advantage of the best zero-cost metric (max of cosine, hyperbolic) over Euclidean, the correlation survives: FS r = −0.31 pooled, −0.33 within hierarchical datasets (NC −0.22, same direction). The same $\hat\delta$ that predicts H−R also predicts COS−R (FS r = −0.39). More tree-like still means more exploitable.
2. **The most tree-like family has the largest downstream gain, not the smallest.** DINOv2's best-zero-cost-metric advantage grows with scale exactly as $\hat\delta$ falls: S→G, +0.6..+0.8pp → +2.4..+2.6pp (FS, hierarchical datasets, per-cell values; the −0.5pp H−COS reported to Reviewer RJje is a family mean, and the cosine gap widens with scale). A near-inversion would require the opposite monotonicity; against Euclidean, the prediction is monotone as in the paper.
3. **The negative prediction is metric-agnostic.** On MNIST, cosine gains +0.2pp on average and residual offsets are uncorrelated with $\hat\delta$: the diagnostic's "no structure to exploit" verdict covers the angular metric too.

What the cosine study genuinely changes, and the revision will state it this plainly, is the metric-selection step: the training objective determines where the tree lives: angular for SSL (sibling triplet agreement 0.91 vs 0.71, cosine vs Euclidean), and at least partly radial for contrastive VLMs, where H beats cosine by +0.9..+1.3pp on CIFAR-100/10/DTD even after L2 normalization (ImageNet +0.1..+0.2pp): signal the Poincaré metric exploits and cosine discards. The zero-cost decision tool becomes two-step, both steps free: (i) $\hat\delta$/ORC decide whether to leave Euclidean at all (flat datasets: no); (ii) the objective type selects the metric (SSL → cosine; contrastive VLM → Poincaré; supervised → either). Contributions 3–4 will be restated in these terms, "predicts when non-Euclidean inter-class structure is exploitable, and which zero-cost metric collects it", rather than as an unqualified hyperbolic advantage.
