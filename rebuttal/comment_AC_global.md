**Global response — summary of new experiments and revised positioning**

We thank the AC and reviewers. During the response period we ran the full set of controls the reviews requested — on the same frozen features, splits and estimators as the paper — and organized our per-review responses around the meta-review's five discussion points. One-paragraph summary of each:

**1. Clarity (1Eqj).** We answer every technical question directly (including a numerical calibration that resolves the absolute-vs-normalized $\delta$ confusion: hyperbolic spaces have bounded absolute $\delta$, hence normalized $\delta/\mathrm{diam}\to0$ with region size, while spheres are scale-invariant at high values) and commit to a concrete revision list: a main-text results table, restructured §2.3/§4.1/§4.4, enlarged Fig. 2, fixed references, and explicit motivation for the two descriptors.

**2. Spherical and matched Euclidean baselines (RJje).** Added: iid Gaussian, uniform-sphere, whitened, dimension-matched (PCA), and spectrum-matched nulls, plus L2/cosine/geodesic variants, for all 12 vision models. Results: forcing centroids onto the sphere *raises* $\hat\delta$ for every model (spherical geometry disfavored); the reviewer's concentration concern is partially correct — raw $\hat\delta$ levels need matched nulls, and under the spectrum-matched null the refined picture is that supervised and contrastive models carry genuine higher-order tree structure while DINOv2's low raw $\hat\delta$ is largely spectrum-driven. We will restate absolute-level and cross-scale claims as excesses over matched nulls. We also concede explicitly that we do not estimate curvature, and will retitle descriptive claims to "tree-like inter-class metric structure".

**3. Robustness to prompts, label groupings, explicit hierarchies (RJje, Xbn5, pux6).** Added: (i) six-template prompt study — text embedders highly robust (range ≤0.011), causal LMs template-sensitive (we will narrow those claims); (ii) random-vs-WordNet grouping contrast — semantic groupings are more tree-like (8/12 models; e.g. CLIP-B .126 vs .174); (iii) ground-truth-hierarchy alignment — centroid distances correlate with WordNet at $\rho$ up to +0.59 (controls ≈0), and CIFAR-100 dendrograms recover the true 20 superclasses at ARI up to 0.39. Notably, the most tree-like family by $\hat\delta$ (label-free DINOv2) is the *least* WordNet-aligned — decoupling "how tree-like" from "whose tree", which bounds the centroid-circularity concern quantitatively.

**4. Downstream beyond prototype tasks (Xbn5, pux6).** Honest scoping: kNN (−2.6pp) and sample retrieval (−3.8pp, 0/60 cells positive) do *not* benefit — the revision will state the domain of validity (prototype-based metric tasks) explicitly. Within that domain the study is now metric-controlled: a matched radial-rescaling control shows the H−R gain is attributable to the hyperbolic metric itself, and against cosine — the strongest zero-cost alternative — the hyperbolic advantage concentrates on contrastive VLMs (CLIP few-shot +0.9..+1.3pp over cosine even after L2 normalization, CI95 ≤ ±0.15pp), while cosine suffices for DINOv2. Few-shot cells now carry per-episode paired CIs (±0.06–0.21pp).

**5. PRH framing (RJje, Xbn5).** We tested the quantitative bridge (cross-model mutual-kNN alignment under Euclidean vs Poincaré): hyperbolic does not improve cross-model alignment (0.474 vs 0.427). We will therefore reposition the contribution as a *within-model* characterization of representation geometry, soften "universal" and the PRH framing, and discuss the recent critical PRH literature raised by RJje.

We believe the paper that results from these controls is more precise and more useful: the descriptive geometry gains proper calibration and a quantitative semantic-correctness measurement; the downstream study gains the controls that isolate the metric effect; and the claims are narrowed to what the evidence supports. Anonymized code for all new experiments is available on request.
