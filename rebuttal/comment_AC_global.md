**Global response — summary of new experiments and revised positioning**

Dear AC: we sincerely thank you and the reviewers for the time and effort invested in our submission. This comment is an executive index of the new experiments reported in our four per-review rebuttals (all posted via the Rebuttal buttons; nothing here is absent from those public responses), keyed to the five discussion points of your initial meta-review. All experiments use the same frozen features, splits and estimators as the paper.

**1. Clarity (1Eqj).** Every technical question answered directly, including a numerical calibration that resolves the absolute-vs-normalized $\delta$ ambiguity our text created. The promised main-text summary table is shown in the response itself, together with a concrete revision list (restructured sections, fixed references, motivated metrics).

**2. Spherical and matched Euclidean baselines (RJje).** All requested controls run (iid Gaussian, sphere, whitened, dimension- and spectrum-matched nulls, L2/cosine variants, 12 models). Sphericizing raises $\hat\delta$ for every model: spherical geometry is disfavored. The concentration concern is partially correct, raw $\hat\delta$ needs matched nulls, and under them **69 of 72 model×dataset cells show genuine tree structure beyond their null**; the only exceptions are DINOv2-S/B/G on ImageNet (its pretraining-like distribution), while the same family is the strongest on transfer datasets, deepening with scale. Claims will be restated as excesses over matched nulls; we concede we do not estimate curvature and retitle descriptive claims to "tree-like inter-class metric structure".

**3. Robustness: prompts, groupings, explicit hierarchies (RJje, Xbn5, pux6).** Six-template prompt study: embedders highly robust, causal LMs template-sensitive (those claims will be narrowed). WordNet-coherent groupings are more tree-like than random ones in 11/12 models. Ground-truth alignment: centroid distances correlate with WordNet at $\rho$ up to +0.59 (controls ≈0) and CIFAR-100 dendrograms recover the true 20 superclasses at ARI up to 0.61. Notably, the most tree-like family by $\hat\delta$ (label-free DINOv2) is the *least* WordNet-aligned, decoupling "how tree-like" from "whose tree" and bounding the centroid-circularity concern.

**4. Downstream beyond prototype tasks (Xbn5, pux6).** Honest scoping: kNN and sample retrieval do *not* benefit (0/60 cells); the domain of validity, prototype-based metric tasks, will be stated explicitly. Within it, a matched rescaling control shows the H−R gain is the metric itself, and against cosine the hyperbolic advantage concentrates on contrastive VLMs (CLIP few-shot +0.9..+1.3pp over cosine even after L2 normalization), while cosine suffices for DINOv2. Statistics added: per-episode paired CIs and McNemar tests (headline cell p ≈ 4×10⁻³⁸). A new text-modality test (DBpedia's 3-level hierarchy, BGE/E5/GTE) shows the diagnostic transfers: above the paper's threshold it correctly predicts no meaningful gain, while the geometry side shows genuine tree excess and 0.88 sibling alignment with the ground-truth hierarchy.

**5. PRH framing (RJje, Xbn5).** We tested the quantitative bridge (cross-model mutual-kNN alignment, Euclidean vs Poincaré): hyperbolic does not improve it (0.474 vs 0.427). This is the position §2.4 of the submission already states ("a within-model property that stands on its own"); the revision promotes it from Related Work into the title/abstract framing, softens "universal", and discusses the recent critical PRH literature raised by RJje. The PRH-adjacent statement that does survive is **convergence in form, not in content**: tree-like structure is near-universal (69/72 cells beyond matched nulls), but each model builds its own tree.

We believe the paper that results from these controls is more precise and more useful, and we emphasize the overall arc: **no measured result in the paper is retracted**; every recalibration sharpens the scope of an interpretation (sharpening, not retraction), which is precisely the narrowing the meta-review asked us to consider. Anonymized code for all new experiments is available on request.
