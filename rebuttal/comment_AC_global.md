**Global response, summary of new experiments and revised positioning**

Dear AC: we sincerely thank you and the reviewers for the time and effort invested in our submission. This comment is an executive index, for your convenience, of the new experiments reported in our four per-review rebuttals (all posted via the Rebuttal buttons, nothing here is absent from those public responses). It is keyed to the five discussion points of your initial meta-review. During the response period we ran the full set of controls the reviews requested, on the same frozen features, splits and estimators as the paper. One-paragraph summary of each point:

**1. Clarity (1Eqj).** We answer every technical question directly (including a numerical calibration that resolves the absolute-vs-normalized $\delta$ ambiguity our text created: hyperbolic spaces have bounded absolute $\delta$, hence normalized $\delta/\mathrm{diam}\to0$ with region size, while spheres are scale-invariant at high values) and commit to a concrete revision list: a main-text results table, restructured §2.3/§4.1/§4.4, enlarged Fig. 2, fixed references, and explicit motivation for the two descriptors.

**2. Spherical and matched Euclidean baselines (RJje).** Added: iid Gaussian, uniform-sphere, whitened, dimension-matched (PCA and random projection), and spectrum-matched nulls, plus L2/cosine/geodesic variants, for all 12 vision models. Results: forcing centroids onto the sphere *raises* $\hat\delta$ for every model (spherical geometry disfavored); the reviewer's concentration concern is partially correct, raw $\hat\delta$ levels need matched nulls. Under spectrum-matched nulls run on all six datasets, **69 of 72 model×dataset cells show genuine tree structure beyond their matched null**; the only exceptions are DINOv2-S/B/G on ImageNet (its pretraining-like distribution), the same family shows the *strongest* genuine excess of the panel on the transfer datasets, deepening with scale. This directly supports the paper's capacity×data thesis, with the ImageNet-level claims restated as excesses over matched nulls. We also concede explicitly that we do not estimate curvature, and will retitle descriptive claims to "tree-like inter-class metric structure".

**3. Robustness to prompts, label groupings, explicit hierarchies (RJje, Xbn5, pux6).** Added: (i) six-template prompt study, text embedders highly robust (range ≤0.011), causal LMs template-sensitive (we will narrow those claims); (ii) random-vs-WordNet grouping contrast, semantic groupings are more tree-like (11/12 models; e.g. CLIP-B .126 vs .174); (iii) ground-truth-hierarchy alignment, centroid distances correlate with WordNet at $\rho$ up to +0.59 (controls ≈0), and CIFAR-100 dendrograms recover the true 20 superclasses at ARI up to 0.61 (Ward linkage; robustness over average/complete/k-means verified). Notably, the most tree-like family by $\hat\delta$ (label-free DINOv2) is the *least* WordNet-aligned, decoupling "how tree-like" from "whose tree", which bounds the centroid-circularity concern quantitatively.

**4. Downstream beyond prototype tasks (Xbn5, pux6).** Honest scoping: kNN (−2.6pp) and sample retrieval (−3.8pp, 0/60 cells positive) do *not* benefit, the revision will state the domain of validity (prototype-based metric tasks) explicitly. Within that domain the study is now metric-controlled: a matched radial-rescaling control shows the H−R gain is attributable to the hyperbolic metric itself, and against cosine, the strongest zero-cost alternative, the hyperbolic advantage concentrates on contrastive VLMs (CLIP few-shot over cosine even after L2 normalization: +0.9..+1.3pp on CIFAR-100/10/DTD, +0.1..+0.2pp on ImageNet; CI95 ≤ ±0.15pp), while cosine suffices for DINOv2. Few-shot cells now carry per-episode paired CIs (±0.03–0.20pp), and NC cells McNemar tests (headline DINOv2-L/CIFAR-100 cell: p ≈ 4×10⁻³⁸). A new text-modality test (DBpedia's 3-level hierarchy, BGE/E5/GTE) shows the diagnostic transfers: at $\hat\delta$ = 0.12–0.13 (above the paper's threshold) it correctly predicts no meaningful gain, while the geometry side shows genuine tree excess and 0.88 sibling alignment with the ground-truth hierarchy.

**5. PRH framing (RJje, Xbn5).** We tested the quantitative bridge (cross-model mutual-kNN alignment under Euclidean vs Poincaré): hyperbolic does not improve cross-model alignment (0.474 vs 0.427). This is the position §2.4 of the submission already states ("a within-model property that stands on its own"); the revision promotes it from Related Work into the title/abstract framing, softens "universal", and discusses the recent critical PRH literature raised by RJje. The PRH-adjacent statement that does survive our controls is **convergence in form, not in content**: tree-like structure is near-universal (69/72 cells beyond matched nulls), but each model builds its own tree.

We believe the paper that results from these controls is more precise and more useful: the descriptive geometry gains proper calibration and a quantitative semantic-correctness measurement; the downstream study gains the controls that isolate the metric effect; and the claims are narrowed to what the evidence supports. We emphasize the overall arc: **no measured result in the paper is retracted**, every recalibration sharpens the scope of an interpretation (sharpening, not retraction), which is precisely the narrowing the meta-review asked us to consider. Anonymized code for all new experiments is available on request.
