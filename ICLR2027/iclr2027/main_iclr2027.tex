\begin{abstract}
Hyperbolic methods for representation learning rest on a premise we call latent hyperbolicity: standard models are already tree-like, because their class geometry scores a low Gromov $\delta$. We show that this raw reading is confounded by dimension, by covariance spectrum and by the supremum statistic itself. We build an instrument that reads every score as an excess over a random cloud with the same dimension and spectrum, ranks that excess against matched null replicates, and adds a depth test whose power and validity regime are measured. Applied to 12 vision backbones, 6 datasets and 16 text models, the instrument gives a nuanced answer. On image features, where the premise is read, the excess sits within null noise in most cells and is small where it survives. On class centroids, clustered structure is genuine in 49 of 72 cells, but it is rarely deeper than a star. The depth test, conservative and weak at ImageNet's noise level, certifies hierarchy above the superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones, and a backbone trained in hyperbolic space shows the same clustering and no detected depth. The trees are moderately shared: a naive comparison isolates the self-supervised family as an island, an artifact of the clustering cut, and once the cut is controlled every training recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision, with the self-supervised tree living in angles rather than distances. Read correctly, foundation models organize classes into clustered structure that is occasionally hierarchical and moderately shared; they do not converge to one common tree, and their raw tree-likeness is not evidence for hyperbolic geometry. % expR62_samplelevel_record.csv, expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR63_meru_record.csv
\end{abstract}

Semantic knowledge is hierarchical, and hyperbolic space is the natural geometric home for hierarchies \citep{nickel2017poincare, sarkar2011low}. A line of work therefore imposes it on representations at training time, from vision to vision--language and language \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, ermolov2022hyperbolic, atigh2022hyperbolic, bdeir2024fully, desai2023meru, pal2025compositional, chen2022fully, he2026helm, sinha2024learning}. These methods cite a premise, which we call \emph{latent hyperbolicity}: standard models are said to organize concepts already in a tree-like metric structure, because the Gromov $\delta$ of their features is low \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully, yang2025hyperbolic, tifrea2019poincare}. Is the premise right, and what does it license?

The raw reading is confounded. Gromov $\delta$ assigns zero to trees \citep{Gromov1987}, but three artifacts push it down without any hierarchy. High-dimensional clouds concentrate toward equidistance, which is a degenerate star tree: we call this the \emph{dimension confound}. Anisotropic spectra mimic low-dimensional behavior: the \emph{spectrum confound}. And the supremum over sampled quadruples is a one-quadruple extreme that does not converge: the \emph{statistic confound}. The three are visible in real models: small GPT-2 models sit at their matched null despite a low raw reading, and classic sentence embedders share their raw reading with vision-contrastive models while carrying none of their structure. % exp18_text_nulls.csv, expR53_text_haar_p999_200.csv

We build the \emph{instrument} that reads $\delta$ correctly. It calibrates the estimator on reference geometries, expresses every reading as an \emph{excess} over a null cloud matched in dimension and covariance spectrum, ranks that excess against matched replicates, and adds a \emph{depth test} whose power and validity regime are measured. We apply it at the sample level, where the premise is read, and at the class level, where hierarchy lives. With it we ask of geometry what convergence work asks of similarity \citep{huh2024platonic, groger2026aristotelian}: what survives calibration, and is it shared? In the words of the title: is there a Platonic tree? They calibrate similarity across models; we calibrate geometry within a model, and comparing trees across models needs a calibration of its own.

\textbf{The answer has three parts.} On image features the excess sits within null noise in most cells and is small where it survives, so the premise does not survive calibration as stated. On class centroids, clustered structure is genuine in 49 of 72 cells, but it is rarely deeper than a star. The depth test, conservative and weak at ImageNet's noise level, certifies hierarchy above the superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones, and a backbone trained in hyperbolic space shows the same clustering and no detected depth. The trees are moderately shared: the naive comparison manufactures an island, and once the clustering cut is controlled every recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision, with the self-supervised structure living in angles. What follows for practice is what to measure before imposing curvature and which zero-cost readout collects what is there. Clustered, occasionally hierarchical, moderately shared: not one common tree, and no license for curvature. % expR62_samplelevel_record.csv, expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR63_meru_record.csv, exp23_treemap_controls.npz

\textbf{Our contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}[topsep=0pt,itemsep=1pt,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The instrument.} Estimator calibration, spectrum-matched nulls read as corrected ranks, and a depth test with measured power; the raw reading misleads at the sample level and at the class level alike.
\item \textbf{The census.} Clustered structure in most models, hierarchy certified in a few, and a hyperbolic backbone as the control for imposing the geometry.
\item \textbf{The map of trees.} The island is an artifact of the cut; sharing is graded and supervision-dependent, and the self-supervised tree is angular.
\item \textbf{Consequences for practice.} What to measure before imposing curvature, and which zero-cost readout collects what is there.
\end{enumerate}
The premise and its confounds have a history, which we review before building the instrument.


\section{Related Work and Background}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{The premise.} Hyperbolic representation learning builds on tree embeddability and Poincar\'{e} embeddings and now spans vision, vision--language, graphs and language \citep{sarkar2011low, nickel2017poincare, ganea2018hyperbolic, chami2019hyperbolic}. Its stated premise rests on a low raw reading for CNN and ViT features, token embeddings and word vectors, taken at the sample level, uncalibrated and under a single construction of the statistic \citep{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR, bdeir2024fully, yang2025hyperbolic, tifrea2019poincare}. Section~\ref{sec:form} re-reads them with the instrument.

\paragraph{Calibrating geometry against nulls.} Network science reads Gromov $\delta$ on real graphs against reference graphs: the curvature of communication and social networks is compared with that of lattices, hyperbolic grids and Erd\H{o}s--R\'{e}nyi random graphs of comparable size, and $\delta$ is read relative to the graph's diameter \citep{narayan2011curvature, kennedy2013hyperbolicity}. \citet{adcock2013treelike} pair $\delta$ with tree decompositions and find the bare value of $\delta$ too coarse a guide to tree-likeness on its own. Reading $\delta$ against a reference is therefore not new. Representation similarity, intrinsic dimension and neural collapse characterize Euclidean structure without testing tree-likeness \citep{kornblith2019cka, ansuini2019intrinsic, pope2021intrinsic, papyan2020prevalence}. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis argues that models converge toward shared representations; \citet{groger2026aristotelian} show that permutation-calibrated similarity removes most of that convergence while local structure survives, and \citet{park2024geometry} read concept hierarchy in language models from orthogonality \citep{huh2024platonic, koepke2026cave}. What is new here is the instrument for embedding clouds rather than graphs: spectrum-matched nulls at the resolution of the null replicate, a depth test against matched stars measured on real clouds, a hyperbolic backbone as the control for imposing the geometry, and a calibration of the tree-to-tree comparison itself.

\paragraph{Background.} For four points $w,x,y,z$ of a metric space, sort the three pairing sums $d(w,x){+}d(y,z)$, $d(w,y){+}d(x,z)$, $d(w,z){+}d(x,y)$ as $S_{\max}\ge S_{\mathrm{mid}}\ge S_{\min}$; the four-point defect is $(S_{\max}-S_{\mathrm{mid}})/2$ and $\delta$ is its supremum. Trees have $\delta=0$, and under this definition the hyperbolic plane has $\delta=\ln 2\approx0.693$, distinct from the thin-triangles constant $\ln(1+\sqrt2)\approx0.881$ \citep{nica2016strong}. We use the normalized $\delta_{\text{norm}}=\delta_{\max}/\mathrm{diam}$ throughout, on which trees $<$ hyperbolic $<$ spherical. Sampled-quadruple estimation has known bias and scale caveats, which the calibration-plus-null design addresses, and Ollivier--Ricci curvature on a kNN graph is the local companion \citep{fournier2015computing, ollivier2009ricci}. How the raw reading becomes a calibrated one is the subject of the next section.

\label{sec:instrument}

If the premise were right, a low raw reading would be evidence on its own. The dimension confound says it is not, so this section builds the measurement layer in whose units every later claim is stated.

\paragraph{The premise lives at the sample level; hierarchy lives at the class level.} Each frozen model is a fixed feature extractor; the premise is read on per-image features, where the literature measures it. Hierarchy is measured on class centroids: in vision, the mean of each class's cached training embeddings, a hundred per class on ImageNet; in text, the embeddings of ``a photo of a \{class\}'' over the ImageNet classes. A \emph{cell} is one pair of model and class set; the census covers 12 vision backbones on 6 datasets and 16 text models. The raw reading is the 99.9th-percentile four-point statistic $\hat\delta_{99.9}$ over $5{\times}10^5$ sampled quadruples per seed, averaged over 10 seeds; the supremum, the pre-registered statistic, is reported in the appendix for the reason given below. The excess is budget-stable (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}).

\paragraph{A low raw reading is not evidence.} Calibrated on reference geometries, the estimator recovers the tree zero and the $\ln 2$ constant of $\mathbb{H}^2$, and spheres stay high. Iid Gaussians, which have no structure at all, drift lower as the dimension grows, from $0.104$ at $d{=}192$ to $0.046$ at $d{=}1536$. That drift is the dimension confound in its purest form: the raw reading of a real backbone must be compared with the reading of a structureless cloud of the same shape (Table~\ref{tab:calibration}). The next paragraph builds that comparison.

\paragraph{The reading is an excess over a matched null.} For every cell we synthesize null clouds with the same $n$, $d$, mean and covariance spectrum. We use the Haar construction throughout: random orthogonal coefficients recombined with the real singular values, so the sample spectrum is exact; the Gaussian-coefficient construction is the alternative reported in the appendix. In plain terms, the spectrum null is a cloud with the same shape as the real one and no structure inside it; the hub null of Section~\ref{sec:form} keeps every cluster of the real cloud and only rearranges where the clusters sit. The \textbf{excess} is the raw reading minus the mean reading of 200 such replicates. The primary evidence is the \emph{percentile rank}, the number of replicates above the real value, and its left-tail $p$. A cell is \emph{genuine} when its Benjamini--Hochberg-corrected $p$ across the census is at most $0.05$; we use the word in this sense only. A negative genuine excess certifies \emph{clustered structure}; the word ``tree-like'' is reserved for the depth test of Section~\ref{sec:form}. A positive excess means less tree-like than chance given the spectrum (Appendix~\ref{app:nulls}).

\paragraph{Two choices changed after pre-registration.} The supremum statistic did not converge under the quadruple budget on ImageNet and disagreed with the 99.9th percentile of the same defects in both directions, so the percentile is the raw reading and the supremum is reported in the appendix. The Gaussian-coefficient null does not reproduce the sample spectrum it claims to match when $n$ is small relative to $d$, and it inflates the excess of low-rank clouds; the Haar null does reproduce it, so we use the Haar null and report both. Rank-based calibration is what gives permutation scores their finite-sample validity, so a $z$-score is kept only as a secondary summary, and the null is aggregation-aware in the sense of \citet[\S5]{groger2026aristotelian}: the same percentile of the same sampled defects is evaluated on every replicate (Table~\ref{tab:b21-2x2}).

\paragraph{Projection and tasks.} A parameter-free map centers the features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$ and preserves directions, so Euclidean, Poincar\'{e} and cosine readouts can be compared on the intact representation; nearest-centroid and 5-way 5-shot classification score them for Section~\ref{sec:practice} (Appendix~\ref{app:corollary}). With the instrument in place, we read the premise where it is read. % exp1, exp2

If standard models were latently hyperbolic, the instrument should find an excess on their image features, where the premise is read. Does it, and does anything survive on class centroids, where hierarchy lives?

\paragraph{The premise does not survive calibration.} We read it first on per-image features, roughly a thousand stratified training images per cell for CIFAR-100 and DTD and all twelve backbones, where the raw reading lies in the low band the literature reports. Calibrated, it is not genuine in 14 of 24 cells, and where it is genuine the excess is small and concentrated in the self-supervised family, and that family's structure is the angular tree of Section~\ref{sec:content}, which cosine collects. A low raw reading on image features is therefore what a random cloud of the same dimension and spectrum produces, up to a small margin. This is the reading that motivates hyperbolic methods, and it does not survive calibration as stated (Table~\ref{tab:census}, last two columns). Class centroids, where hierarchy lives, read differently, and we turn to them next. % expR62_samplelevel_record.csv

\paragraph{Clustered structure is the rule.} Figure~\ref{fig:excess} and Table~\ref{tab:census} give the census of record. Almost every cell sits below its matched null, and in 49 of the 72 the gap is one that chance does not produce. On ImageNet and CIFAR-100, the two sets with a real hierarchy, the count is 18 of 24. The structure belongs to no single family or architecture: every family contributes genuine cells, two self-supervised ResNets read the same as the ViTs, and resampling the images behind each centroid does not move the reading. What does move it is the label set: the smallest backbone is genuine on one dataset only, the flat datasets sit at the resolution limit of ten classes, and a class-count control shows that the reading follows the hierarchy of the labels rather than their number (Appendix~\ref{app:csweep}). A star, however, would pass this test too, which is the subject of the next paragraph. % expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR59_imagenet_bootstrap_summary.csv, expR45_convnet_rows.csv, expR60_c_sweep_record.csv

\paragraph{The count survives resampling.} The census null accounts for replicate variability but not for image resampling or the estimator's seeds. Folding all three into one spread, or repeating the census on thirty resampled centroid sets, leaves between 42 and 47 of the 72 cells genuine. On ImageNet and CIFAR-100 the count stays between 17 and 18 of the 24, and the cells that move are on the transfer sets (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}). % expR66_joint_sensitivity_summary.csv

\paragraph{A star passes the census.} The excess measures structure beyond second moments, and a pure star already has such structure. A synthetic star of thirty clusters with no hierarchy scores an excess as deep as any real cell. We call this the \emph{star caveat}. The excess therefore measures how classes are arranged, not the geometry they are embedded in, and a negative excess says clustered, not deep. Whether the arrangement is deeper than a star is a separate question, which needs a test of its own (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). That test is the subject of the next paragraph. % expR42_star_calibration.csv

\paragraph{Depth is certified on ImageNet and not on CIFAR-100.} The depth test compares the real centroids with a matched star: Gaussian hubs at the real hub radius and, within each cluster, a Haar sample with the cluster's own covariance, both read under a null that randomizes the hubs and keeps every cluster intact. Because the null keeps the clusters, the test certifies hierarchy above the labelled clusters only. On ImageNet the real cloud is more hierarchical than its matched star in 4 of 12 backbones, three supervised ViTs and DINOv2-L, and never less. The other eight are not detected, which is not the same as not hierarchical. For the four certified backbones the same clusters with random hubs raise no alarm in any zero-strength run, 0 of 20, so the verdict comes from their real hub arrangement, which the test reads as deeper than the implanted two-level tree, as Appendix~\ref{app:star} shows. The certified set shifts with the choice of frame, as Table~\ref{tab:b37-implanted} shows. CIFAR-100, with clusters of a few points, lies outside the regime the test covers and its readings stay unvalidated. An earlier isotropic star produced false alarms and is withdrawn (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}a). How much depth the test can see on real clouds is the subject of the next paragraph. % expR56_depth_variants.csv, expR55b_depth_power_leafframe.csv, expR55_depth_power.csv

\paragraph{On real clouds the test is conservative and weak.} We measured the test on the real ImageNet clouds themselves, replacing the hub arrangement by an implanted two-level tree of graded strength while keeping every within-cluster offset, and the census cannot tell the implanted tree from the star. The test raises no false alarm, declaring none of the sixty zero-strength runs hierarchical. A clean implanted tree is detected in none of the twelve backbones, and the misses are a matter of noise level: the within-cluster spread of the real clouds is 1.3 to 3.9 times their between-hub spread, the synthetic sweep covered ratios below one, and once the real offsets are shrunk into that range every backbone is detected, and two contrastive backbones also fire at zero strength, so the shrunk variant is a diagnostic of power, not a substitute test. The test is therefore conservative and weak at ImageNet's noise level: its positive verdicts stand, and a backbone it does not certify is not thereby flat. A trained control, a ViT-B fine-tuned with a hierarchical objective, is inconclusive, as Appendix~\ref{app:star} reports (Figure~\ref{fig:depth}b). Whether imposing the geometry creates detectable depth is the next question. % expR64b_wn30.csv, expR64b_wn30_summary.csv, expR65_hier_finetune.csv

\paragraph{Imposing the geometry does not create depth.} MERU embeds images and text on the Lorentz hyperboloid with an entailment objective, and its released Euclidean twin is a CLIP baseline with the same backbone, data and recipe minus the hyperbolic lift, so the pair isolates the geometry \citep{desai2023meru}. Passed through the instrument unchanged, the two read the same excess on ImageNet images, and MERU's reading in its own Lorentz metric equals its Euclidean reading to the fourth decimal. Under the depth test, conservative and weak at this noise level, neither model is detected as more hierarchical than a matched star, and MERU is never ahead of its twin. Training in hyperbolic space therefore neither creates the clustering nor adds detectable depth (Table~\ref{tab:b30-meru}). If the geometry does not explain the clustering, the training objective might, and neural collapse is the first candidate. % expR63_meru_record.csv

\paragraph{Neural collapse is the flat limit, not the reading.} Neural collapse predicts that the class means of a supervised classifier converge to a simplex equiangular tight frame \citep{papyan2020prevalence}. An exact frame is the extreme case of the star caveat, $\delta$ zero with no hierarchy at all, and our reading differs from it in two ways. The arrangement we certify is not flat, because class means cluster around superclass hubs that a frame would erase, and cutting the centroid dendrograms recovers the CIFAR-100 superclasses. The same clustered structure also appears in models trained without class labels, on transfer datasets and in causal language models, outside the setting where neural collapse is stated, and the depth test asks what neural collapse does not, whether there is hierarchy above the hubs, answering yes for four ImageNet backbones (Appendix~\ref{app:orc}). What moves the excess, if not the objective's flat limit, is scale and training, which we take up last. % night/orc_bridges.csv

\paragraph{Scale deepens the excess for one family only.} Figure~\ref{fig:excess}b shows that scale does not deepen the excess in general, with one exception: DINOv2, whose excess deepens with size on CIFAR-10 and, weakly, on CIFAR-100. Training moves the raw reading within an architecture: fine-tuning on a task with no class hierarchy raises it, it falls across transformer depth, and shuffling the embedding-to-label correspondence leaves it unchanged (Appendix~\ref{app:interventions}). Together, these results say that what survives at the class level is clustered structure that is genuine in most cells, rarely deep, and not created by the geometry of the embedding space. Whether that structure is shared across models, and whose tree it is, is the subject of the next section. % expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, analysis4_finetuning.csv, e1_delta_by_layer.csv


\section{Findings II: Whose Tree}
\label{sec:content}

If the measured trees were a shared representation of reality, models should agree with each other and with human taxonomy. Whether they appear to depends on how the trees are measured.

\paragraph{The naive map manufactures an island.} We extract each model's hierarchy as a dendrogram over the shared centroids and compare hierarchies by the adjusted Rand index between cut partitions and by the cophenetic correlation between tree and distances. Under the naive configuration, Euclidean distances with average linkage, the map is spectacular: supervised ViTs and contrastive VLMs form one block, and the label-free DINOv2 family appears as an island that agrees with no supervised model. We call this the \emph{island}. Its cuts are degenerate: DINOv2's Euclidean dendrograms chain, almost every class falls into one cluster, and the index is forced to zero by construction. A configuration must therefore be selected by a criterion fixed without reference to the agreements being compared: we require the highest cophenetic fidelity among configurations whose reference cuts are non-degenerate for every model, which selects cosine-average on ImageNet and cosine-complete on CIFAR-100. The criterion is applied per dataset, because degeneracy is a property of the dataset, and it never sees the agreements it selects for. Under every non-degenerate configuration the gap stays moderate and never returns to the island, and the configuration it selects on DBpedia is the one that degenerates on ImageNet, which is why it cannot be transferred. Figure~\ref{fig:treemap} shows the map before and after this control (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). Under the selected configurations the island has to be re-read. % exp22_tree_similarity_{imagenet,cifar100}.npz, exp23_treemap_controls.npz, exp23_config_diagnostics.json

\paragraph{The island is an artifact, and sharing is graded.} Under the selected configurations three measures agree. At the cut, the island shrinks from categorical to moderate: DINOv2 agrees with the block at 0.38 against 0.48 within the block. In cophenetic correlation a gap remains. In triplet agreement, which needs no cut, the gap closes entirely under angular configurations and persists under Euclidean ones. Across every non-degenerate configuration, label-free models recover the human taxonomy partially and supervision deepens the resemblance: paired within each admissible configuration, the label-supervised ViT recovers more than each DINOv2 model in 11 of 12 comparisons (Appendix~\ref{app:treemap}). The gap is a property of the measure as much as of the models, and its persistence under Euclidean measures alone points to where the self-supervised structure lives. % exp23_treemap_controls.npz, expR58_treemap_cutfree_summary.csv, exp28_recovery_per_config.csv

\paragraph{The self-supervised tree lives in angles.} DINOv2's semantics concentrate in the angular component of its representation: for DINOv2-L, sibling triplets are resolved far better under cosine than under Euclidean distance. We call this structure the \emph{angular tree}. It is why DINOv2's Euclidean dendrograms chain into the island and why the triplet gap closes only under angular configurations. The census agrees when we repeat it on cosine geometry, with L2-normalized centroids, geodesic distances and the same Haar null on the normalized cloud: the verdict matches the Euclidean census in 65 of 72 cells, and DINOv2 on ImageNet is genuine under both readings. We report the cosine census as robustness, never as the record (Table~\ref{tab:b32-cosine}). Where the structure lives decides which readout collects it, the concern of Section~\ref{sec:practice}. First we ask whose taxonomy the trees recover. % exp10_local_vs_global.csv, expR57_census_cosine_haar_p999_200.csv

\paragraph{WordNet alignment follows supervision, not tree-likeness.} The correlation between inter-centroid distances and WordNet tree distances on ImageNet orders the families by supervision: highest for the contrastive VLMs, then the supervised ViTs, and lowest for DINOv2, with shuffle and Gaussian controls at zero. DINOv2 is nevertheless genuine on ImageNet and both CIFAR sets, and cutting centroid dendrograms recovers the true CIFAR-100 superclasses at an adjusted Rand index of up to 0.61, against zero for permuted labels. Across the three families, WordNet content and robustness of the excess anti-correlate: the supervised and contrastive families are the most aligned and the most fragile, DINOv2 the least aligned and the most robust (Table~\ref{tab:census}). Alignment with the human taxonomy is therefore something training puts in, not something tree-likeness reveals, which raises the question of circularity. % exp3_alignment.csv, exp8_p1_recovery.csv

\paragraph{The recovery is not WordNet circularity.} ImageNet classes are WordNet synsets, so recovering WordNet there could be circular. Three controls bound the circularity. WordNet-coherent groupings read a lower raw $\delta$ than random ones in 11 of 12 models. Single-image distances still correlate with WordNet, up to a correlation of $+0.26$. And the decoupling of alignment from robustness is the opposite of what label injection would produce. Repeating the tree map on DBpedia Classes, a three-level ontology independent of WordNet, closes the door: no configuration degenerates, the three text embedders recover the true level-2 partition in every configuration, and they agree with each other (Table~\ref{tab:a9}). The recovered hierarchy is real, shared and configuration-robust, and the fragility above belongs to the family and the concept set together, which text makes explicit. % exp1, exp8_p6_pooling.csv, exp27_dbpedia_treemap.json

\paragraph{In text, clustered structure depends on recipe, scale and probe.} On DBpedia, a real three-level text hierarchy, the sentence embedders read a genuine excess with every replicate above the real value. On the harder probe, the thousand ImageNet class names, the same embedders are not genuine, despite raw readings indistinguishable from vision backbones whose excess is. Within causal language models on the class-name probe, the two largest GPT-2 models are genuine under every construction of the null and statistic and under cosine, whereas the two smallest flip across readings: robust at the larger scales, fragile at the smaller. Pythia and OLMo-1B are genuine at every scale tested, and extraction protocol and prompt template move the fragile values but not the scale ordering (Appendix~\ref{app:textcensus}). The trees, then, are shared in content only partially, and whether they are shared in local structure at all is the last question of this section. % expR53_text_haar_p999_200.csv, expR53_text_haar_sup_200.csv, expR53_text_gauss_p999_200.csv, expR48b_text_census200_bs1.csv, expR57_text_cosine_haar_p999_200.csv, expR61_dbpedia_record.csv

\paragraph{Local agreement survives calibration.} We apply the permutation calibration of \citet{groger2026aristotelian} to the 66 model pairs: permutations of the class correspondence give the null distribution, its upper quantile gives the threshold, and the calibrated score is the excess over that threshold rescaled to keep the maximum at one. Mutual-kNN agreement survives calibration with every pair significant, and so does centroid CKA, while the Poincar\'{e} reading improves neither: centroids distill a relational base that models share locally, in who is near whom, even where their trees differ (Table~\ref{tab:b31-groger}). Together, these findings give the thesis its short form. Clustered, occasionally hierarchical, moderately shared: not one common tree, and no license for curvature. % exp21b_local_global_K200.csv

\section{Consequences for Imposing Curvature}
\label{sec:practice}

If the raw reading cannot be read on its own, what should a practitioner read before imposing curvature? And if the structure that survives is clustered and angular, which readout collects it?

\paragraph{The raw reading cannot select a curvature.} \citet{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR} set the curvature of the Poincar\'{e} ball from the measured relative hyperbolicity as $c=(0.144/\delta_{\text{rel}})^2$ with $\delta_{\text{rel}}=2\delta/\mathrm{diam}$. Since the raw reading is dominated by the dimension and spectrum confounds, such a curvature tracks the backbone's width rather than its hierarchy. Structureless Gaussian clouds would receive a curvature from $0.48$ at $d{=}192$ to $2.5$ at $d{=}1536$. The excess is the quantity to read instead (Table~\ref{tab:census}).

\paragraph{The excess is small in absolute terms and large against the null.} On ImageNet centroids it runs from $-0.005$ to $-0.020$. That is 1 to 36 times the null's standard deviation at that class count. On image features it sits within noise in most cells. What to read instead is the next paragraph. % expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR62_samplelevel_record.csv % exp1_delta_controls.csv, expR52_census_haar_p999_200.csv, expR62_samplelevel_record.csv

\paragraph{Measure before imposing.} For a frozen backbone the instrument answers three questions in minutes. Is there structure beyond a spectrum-matched null? The excess and its rank say. Is it deeper than a star? The depth test says, conservative and weak at ImageNet's noise level, so that a positive verdict certifies and a negative one says nothing. Where does it live, in angles or in distances? The cosine reading of Section~\ref{sec:content} says. In this census the answers are usually yes, rarely, and, for the self-supervised family, in angles, and a change of readout collects that structure at no cost.

\paragraph{A zero-cost readout collects what is there.} Changing the readout metric on the intact representation costs nothing, and cosine collects the angular tree. The Poincar\'{e} readout adds from $+0.1$ to $+1.3$ pp over cosine for the contrastive VLMs on prototype tasks. For the other families its effect is inconsistent, from $-1.7$ to $+1.1$ pp. On flat-label datasets no metric helps, and sample-level tasks never benefit. The full study is in Appendix~\ref{app:corollary}, and the discussion states the three acts together. % exp2_metric_controls.csv, exp2b_normalized_stack.csv, exp24_val_metric_selection.csv

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the paper establishes.} Read correctly, foundation models organize classes into clustered structure that is occasionally hierarchical and moderately shared; they do not converge to one common tree, and their raw tree-likeness is not evidence for hyperbolic geometry. In three acts: the premise of latent hyperbolicity is confounded and its sample-level reading sits within null noise; what survives at the class level is clustered structure in 49 of 72 cells, hierarchy above the superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones, no detected depth from imposing the geometry, and a moderately shared, supervision-dependent recovery of the human taxonomy, angular for the self-supervised family; and what follows is that curvature should be measured rather than assumed and a zero-cost readout collects what is there.

\paragraph{Open questions.} Why the structure is clustered but rarely certified as deep, and why the self-supervised tree is angular, are open: every recipe recovers the superclass hubs, yet only four backbones place them detectably beyond a star.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~The excess certifies structure beyond second moments, star-like clustering included; the depth test certifies hierarchy above its frame only, is conservative and weak at ImageNet's noise level, leaves hierarchy within the clusters untested, and its one trained positive control is inconclusive (Appendix~\ref{app:star}). (ii)~The census is class-centroid geometry on the datasets of Table~\ref{tab:census}. (iii)~Causal-LM values, not their scale ordering, are template-conditioned. (iv)~The objective--geometry link is correlational and rests chiefly on DINOv2's scale range, as Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr} shows. (v)~Downstream scope is prototype-based metric tasks with modest gains. (vi)~The descriptor $\xi$ tracks norm dispersion rather than curvature, as Appendix~\ref{app:xi} reports. (vii)~A causal LM's class representation depends on the extraction protocol as much as on the model. (viii)~The sample-level reading covers two datasets and one image budget.

\subsection{The corollary: diagnosing exploitability}
\label{app:corollary}
\paragraph{One parameter-free projection and two tasks serve the corollary.} The parameter-free map $\phi(x)=\tanh(\tfrac12\|s(x-\mu)\|)\cdot\frac{s(x-\mu)}{\|s(x-\mu)\|}$ centers features, scales the 95th-percentile norm to radius $1/\sqrt2$, and preserves directions; prototypes in the ball are means of projected points re-mapped through the origin. Tasks: nearest-centroid (NC) and 5-way 5-shot (FS, 1000 paired episodes); metric arms: Euclidean (R), Poincar\'{e} (H), the radial map with Euclidean distances (RT), cosine (COS), and the L2-normalized stack. Changing the readout metric on the intact configuration, which is what the rule selects among, is distinct from deforming the configuration itself. % exp1, exp2

\paragraph{$\delta_{\text{norm}}$ predicts the gain within datasets.} Within each dataset the Poincar\'{e} advantage tracks $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ (NC $r=-0.83$ on ImageNet) and survives partialing out dimension, baseline accuracy, family and spectral covariates; without DINOv2 it holds on ImageNet and CIFAR-100 but not on CIFAR-10/DTD (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). Pooled cross-dataset correlations are family-driven and not used as evidence; on flat-label datasets no metric helps. Gains carry paired confidence intervals and a McNemar test (Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}). % table1_regenerated, exp20, night/correlation_cis, exp13

\paragraph{The training objective picks the metric.} The radial map with Euclidean distances changes nothing, so the gain is the metric itself, and the split follows the objective: cosine suffices for DINOv2, Poincar\'{e} $\approx$ cosine for supervised ViTs, and Poincar\'{e} beats cosine for CLIP by up to $+1.3$pp even after L2 normalization (Figure~\ref{fig:bestmetric} in Appendix~\ref{app:familycorr}; held-out selection and policy comparison in this appendix). The rule is read off the families it is scored on: it describes them rather than validating a policy. % exp2, exp2b, exp24

\paragraph{A diagnostic, not a policy.} \citet{Khrulkov_2020_CVPR} estimate $c\approx0.33$ from the raw reading of their datasets; the same rule on structureless Gaussian clouds gives the curvatures of Section~\ref{sec:practice}. Cosine is a safe default, gating on $\delta_{\text{norm}}$ is the weakest policy tested (this appendix), and gains are modest and limited to prototype-based tasks (sample-level tasks never benefit, Appendix~\ref{app:sample}). On DBpedia, a three-level text hierarchy, the diagnostic predicts correctly on both sides: genuine excess, no metric gain. % exp2, exp20, exp24, exp14, exp15

\subsection{Parallelogram defect $\xi$: a norm-structure descriptor}
\label{app:xi}
\paragraph{$\xi$ reads norm structure, not curvature.} A parallelogram estimator $\xi$, intended as a curvature sign, is driven negative by heterogeneous norms alone, so it is reported only as a norm-structure descriptor. % exp12, expR43_xi_norm_references.csv
For a triangle $(a,b,c)$ with $m$ the approximate midpoint of $(b,c)$ (the minimizer of $D(\cdot,b){+}D(\cdot,c){+}|D(\cdot,b){-}D(\cdot,c)|$), the parallelogram defect is $\xi = \big(D(a,m)^2 + \tfrac14 D(b,c)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,b)^2 - \tfrac12 D(a,c)^2\big)\,/\,(2\,D(a,m)\,\mathrm{diam})$, averaged over $2{\times}10^4$ sampled triangles: $0$ in Euclidean space, positive on spheres, negative in hyperbolic space and on trees.
