# The paper in ten lines (as it now reads, final pass)

1. Hyperbolic representation learning rests on a premise, latent hyperbolicity: standard models are already tree-like because their class geometry scores a low Gromov δ.
2. That raw reading is confounded three times over, by dimension, by covariance spectrum and by the supremum statistic, and each confound pushes δ down with no hierarchy present.
3. We build an instrument that reads every score as an excess over a random cloud of the same dimension and spectrum, ranks it against 200 matched replicates, and adds a depth test whose false-alarm rate and power are measured on real clouds.
4. Read on image features, where the premise is read, the excess sits within null noise in most cells and reaches at most 40% of the null where it survives: the premise does not survive calibration as stated.
5. Read on class centroids, clustered structure is genuine in 49 of 72 cells and in every family, but a pure star already produces such an excess, so clustered does not mean deep.
6. The depth test certifies hierarchy above the superclasses in 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones, the same four under a star whose hubs carry the real hubs' spectrum, and it never fires on real clouds with randomized hubs; on real clouds it is conservative and weak, so a backbone it does not certify is not thereby flat.
7. A backbone trained in hyperbolic space lives in the near-flat regime of the hyperboloid, reads the same clustering as its Euclidean twin and no detected depth: imposing the geometry creates nothing at the class level, and leaf-label supervision can produce the depth (augreg) or not (DeiT).
8. Comparing the models' trees naively isolates the DINOv2 family as an island; the island is a chaining artifact of the clustering cut, and once the cut is controlled a gap of about a third remains, every recipe recovers the human taxonomy partially, more so with supervision.
9. The self-supervised structure is an angular tree: it lives in angles rather than distances, which is why Euclidean cuts break it and why cosine collects it.
10. Read correctly, foundation models organize classes into clustered structure that is occasionally hierarchical and moderately shared; they do not converge to one common tree, and their raw tree-likeness is not evidence for hyperbolic geometry. Measure the excess, the depth and where the structure lives before imposing curvature, and let a zero-cost readout collect what is there.
