# The paper in ten lines (as the final version reads, `main_iclr2027_final.pdf`; regenerated 2026-09-23 after the plain-language pass)

1. Hyperbolic representation learning rests on a premise, latent hyperbolicity: standard models are already tree-like because their class geometry scores a low Gromov δ, a measure of tree-likeness that is zero for a tree.
2. That low value is also what a random cloud of the same dimension and spread scores: the raw reading is confounded by dimension, by the covariance spectrum and by the supremum statistic, so it cannot be called low on its own.
3. The instrument reads every score as its excess over 200 random clouds with exactly the same centered spectrum (the centered Haar null), ranks it among them, and calls a cell genuine, more than chance would give, under Benjamini–Hochberg at 5%; a depth test with measured power and false-alarm rate reads structure above the superclass centers, the hubs, within a grouping into superclasses, the frame.
4. On image features, where the premise is read, the calibrated reading is indistinguishable from a random cloud in most model–dataset cells and small elsewhere; the four values published by Khrulkov et al. are reproduced within 0.03 and, calibrated, two of them are indistinguishable from a random cloud.
5. Class centroids carry structure that a random cloud of the same shape does not, in 44 of 72 cells and 30 of 36 on the datasets with 47 classes or more, but so does a star of clusters.
6. In 4 of 12 ImageNet backbones the depth test finds structure, but it is the orientation of clusters relative to the superclass centers, not a hierarchy among those centers: once the cluster orientations are randomized the verdicts vanish, whereas a planted hierarchy, synthetic or trained in, survives; the alignment is relational, not radial, and its geometric form is not resolved.
7. Where a planted hierarchy is detected, in nine backbones, none as strong as the planted one is found; in the other three the test cannot tell. Leaf-label supervision can produce the alignment (augreg ViT-B) or not (DeiT-B); MERU's objective does not create hub structure, and its space stays nearly flat.
8. The usual tree comparison makes the self-supervised models look like outliers, an artifact of how trees are cut into groups; compared properly, the trees agree on which classes group together well above chance (triplet agreement 0.77 against a within-model ceiling of 0.85–0.96), though short of what two readings of the same model reach, and not on the distances between them.
9. The self-supervised models organize classes by direction rather than by distance; text depends on recipe and scale (7 of 15 text models genuine on the class names); models share who is near whom and differ in the metric.
10. Read correctly, foundation models organize classes into clusters, agree on how those clusters nest well above chance, though not as much as two readings of one model, and not on the distances between them, show no hierarchy among the superclasses where the test can see one, and their raw tree-likeness is not evidence for hyperbolic geometry.
